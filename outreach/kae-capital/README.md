## The firm

Mumbai and Bengaluru. Founded 2012 by **Sasha Mirchandani**. Pre-seed to pre-Series A,
sector-agnostic. ~$250M managed across three funds, 116 portfolio companies, 31 people
including 9 partners.

### Fund I is the thing worth knowing

Launched 2012, 32 companies. As of the November 2025 announcement:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| India vehicle | **3.6x DPI** (as of Sept 2023) |
| Overseas vehicle | tracking **5x+ DPI** |
| Follow-on capital catalysed | $900M+ |
| Enterprise value generated | $2.7B |
| Described as | "one of the strongest maiden fund performances by a home-grown venture capital firm" |

Two companies drove it. **Porter returned more than 2x of Fund I on its own. HealthKart
returned the entire fund on its own**, with more expected.

> "Fund I's DPI is not just a number, it's a symbol of what's possible when early
> conviction meets enduring partnership." — Gaurav Chaturvedi, General Partner

### And here is the tension worth writing about

**Fund IV is being raised now — $100–110M**, with 80–85% reserved for seed and Series A.
The stated focus, in their own words to press: consumer, fintech, deeptech, B2B and B2C
AI, intelligent automation, cybersecurity, defence, aerospace. Coverage of the Fund I
result says the firm is "sharpening its focus on AI, intelligent automation, manufacturing
resilience, and deeptech."

**Healthcare is not on that list.** And yet the single investment that returned their
maiden fund was a health company.

That is a real, checkable, non-obvious observation, and it is the most interesting thing
Nikita can open with. It is not a criticism — funds are allowed to rotate — it's a
question about how they're thinking, which is exactly the kind of question a partner
answers.

### Healthcare in the portfolio

(AI companion app for tier II/III with health courses and **real-time adherence support**)

## Why she has something real to say to a seed fund

Not the portfolio size. The **pipeline**.

- **30–50 proposals screened a month against a 20–30 parameter framework**, then deep-dive
  on what survives. That is deal flow triage at a volume most seed associates never touch,
  and it is the single most transferable thing on her CV to a fund doing 8–10 new
  investments a year.
- **Assessing founders and institutional leaders directly** on capability, domain knowledge
  and capacity to deploy capital they've never had before — explicitly modelled on
  pre-investment diligence.
- **₹450 crore ($51M) under management** across 40+ programmes, with financial gatekeeping
  on every partner budget in the theme before it reached CEO and Board approval.
- **Health-tech and AI diagnostics as owned sub-themes** — she has already formed views on
  which health technologies were ready to be deployed and which were a demo.

The healthcare domain is the differentiator, but the pipeline is the qualification. A
generalist seed fund hires on judgement and throughput; it keeps the domain depth as the
reason to hire *this* person rather than another one.

## Before she sends

1. **Send Tuesday–Thursday morning IST.** Not Friday, and not while Fund IV is closing if
   that becomes public — a partner mid-raise has no calendar.
2. **No CV attached to the first touch.** Her LinkedIn is the CV at this stage.
3. **Verify the HealthKart line before quoting it.** It comes from Business Standard's
   November 2025 report on Fund I. If the number is wrong the whole letter loses its
   footing — this is the one fact that has to be right.
4. **Don't mention Unitus.** Her path is the reason the letter works, not its subject.
5. **If Sunitha doesn't reply in ~10 days**, go to Vidushi Kamani (Operating Partner)
   rather than up to Sasha. Platform partners at seed funds know what the team needs before
   a role is posted, and Sasha's inbox is the worst-odds door in the firm.
6. **Expect this to be slow.** A fund raising Fund IV is not hiring loudly. The realistic
   outcome is a conversation now and a role in three to six months — which is how most
   venture seats get filled anyway.
