## Don't chase this role

**Growth & Operations Associate.** 70% marketing, 30% ops. The stated bar:

- 3–4 years **at a D2C startup with growth and performance marketing experience**
- Hands-on with a CRM and an ad platform (Meta/Google)
- A/B testing across landing pages and ads, Mixpanel or similar
- Owns CAC, CPL and ROAS
- Tagged **Entry level**, reporting to a Lead

Nikita has none of the five. The nearest thing on her CV is Tiff.in — 520% YouTube and
370% Instagram growth — which was organic content growth at a tiny incubated startup four
years ago, not paid performance marketing. Scored properly this lands around **FIT 2.6**,
below the rubric's "don't" line, and it is an entry-level associate title against a
Program Officer running a ₹450 crore portfolio.

Asking to be considered for it anchors her at exactly the wrong level with someone worth
knowing.

## Why the person is still worth writing to

**Anjali Menon — "Building in healthcare | ex-McKinsey | Berkeley Haas."** That is not a
recruiter. That is an operator posting her own roles, which means the company is early
enough that she is the hiring decision.

And Evervie is described as "a healthcare platform built for specialty care, spanning
**renal care, oncology, and diagnostics**, with new specialty and digital D2C healthcare
platforms **on the horizon**."

Two of those three are areas Nikita has evaluated directly — oncology sits in her
"also evaluated" list, and diagnostics runs through her health-tech and AI diagnostics
sub-themes. "New platforms on the horizon" is a company that will need someone who can
assess which specialty to enter and on what evidence. That is her actual job.

**Do not claim renal care.** It is not in her materials.

An ex-McKinsey founder will read "screens 30–50 proposals a month, ₹450 crore portfolio,
evaluated oncology and diagnostics" instantly and correctly. She is the ideal reader for
this profile.
