# 2070 Health — warm intro to Gaurav Porwal, for Associate, New Ventures

Drafted 2026-10-08, at Nikita's request. The route is **an acquaintance of hers who knows
Gaurav Porwal, but not well.** That single fact determines the whole shape of this, so it
is worth being precise about where the weak link actually is.

**The weak link is the connector to Gaurav, not the connector to Nikita.** Her contact can
still say "I know Nikita and she is worth your time." What they cannot do is spend
relationship capital they do not have, which means:

- **Do not ask them to push, chase or follow up.** One forward, and that is the whole favour.
- **The note has to earn the reply on its own**, because the relationship will not carry it.
- **Give them a genuine way out.** A loose contact who feels cornered either sends a flat
  note that does more harm than none, or quietly does nothing and then avoids her.

Everything below is built so the connector's job is to press forward once.

## Step 1 · Her email to the connector

Keep this short. The length of the ask signals how big the favour is.

**Subject:** A small ask about 2070 Health

> Hi [name],
>
> A small ask, and a genuinely easy one to say no to.
>
> 2070 Health, the healthcare venture studio behind W Health, has a New Ventures role open in
> Mumbai. It is the closest thing to my last four years that I have found, and I would like
> to be considered properly rather than through the form. I remembered you know Gaurav
> Porwal, their COO.
>
> If you are comfortable passing my name on, I have put two things below: a couple of lines
> you can send him, and a note from me underneath that you can forward as it is. Nothing in
> it needs you to vouch for anything you have not seen.
>
> And if you do not know him well enough for this to feel natural, please just say so. I
> would much rather ask than assume, and it will not be awkward either way.
>
> Thank you for considering it.
>
> Nikita

**Why it is built this way:** it names the favour, bounds it at one forward, hands over the
words so the connector does not have to compose anything, and makes declining the path of
least resistance rather than the awkward option. **A loose contact who is given an easy no
is far more likely to say yes.**

## Step 2 · The lines for the connector to send

Written in their voice, deliberately short, and it does not claim closeness to Gaurav.

> Hi Gaurav, hope all is well.
>
> I know Nikita Sachanandani, who has spent the last four years running a healthcare
> portfolio at Tata Trusts, and she is keen on the New Ventures role at 2070 Health. She
> struck me as someone worth seeing directly rather than leaving in an application pile, so I
> said I would pass her note on. It is below, and she is copied here.
>
> Entirely up to you whether you take it any further.

**The last line is the important one.** It releases Gaurav from any obligation, which is
exactly what a loosely connected introducer should do. An intro that implies a debt from a
near-stranger is the kind that gets ignored politely.

### Why this note and not the Jethwani one

**The middle paragraph is doing the work**, and it is deliberately the paragraph most
candidates would leave out. Anyone can say they screened proposals. "I chose districts that
limited confounding factors because I needed to know whether it would survive a system" is a
sentence an operator recognises, and it is true.

**The closing question is the other half.** It is small, specific, answerable in one line,
and only he can answer it. A question like that converts an introduction into a reply,
whereas "I would welcome the chance to discuss further" gives him nothing to type.

### Two things she can add, and one she should not

1. **Availability, if she wants it in.** She left the Trusts on 25 September and can start
   immediately. For a studio with a req that has been open a long time that is a real
   advantage. One line before the sign off does it: *"I am free to start straight away, so
   timing is not a constraint on my side."* Left out of the draft deliberately, consistent
   with how the Beyond email was handled, so the choice stays hers.
2. **A portfolio company**, if she has read one properly. One specific sentence about Elevate
   Now, Nivaan, Hoola Health, Everhope Oncology, Everbright Health or Reveal HealthTech would
   sit well. **Only if she has actually read it.** A generic compliment about the portfolio is
   worse than nothing.
3. **Do not raise valuation or financial modelling.** The JD asks for proficiency in
   valuation and she does not have DCFs, comparables or cap tables. If it comes up in
   conversation she should answer it straight. Volunteering a weakness in an intro email is
   not honesty, it is just a bad first impression, and the honest version belongs in the
   interview where she can show what she does have.
