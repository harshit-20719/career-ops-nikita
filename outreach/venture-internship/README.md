# Breaking into venture — the shortlist, the people, and the messages

Built 2026-10-09 at Nikita's request: *"I really want to break into the venture space, mainly
to learn from it. I want to pitch myself as an intern, someone coming in to learn the for
profit side of things instead of the nonprofit."* **Hyderabad and Bangalore prioritised.**

**Why this is a sound idea even though I argued against an intern seat an hour ago.** The
AlgoUniversity call was that an intern seat at an edtech buys her nothing she does not
already have. **Venture is the opposite case.** Indian healthcare VC produced **zero posted
roles across seven funds** when it was scanned on 2026-09-07, and every deal-side role she
has found came from her own feed or a link she sent. In a lane with no front door, a
fixed-term seat is not a step down, **it is the only door.** And it buys the one thing four
years at Tata Trusts cannot: commercial deal exposure, which is the exact gap that has shown
up in every fund JD she has read.

So the strategy is right. **One word in it is wrong**, and that is covered below.

---

## First, the positioning problem. Do not use the word "intern".

**In India "intern" means student.** At four years and three months, with a ₹450 crore
portfolio and funding recommendations approved by a Board of Trustees, the word does three
things she does not want:

1. It invites the question *"why would someone with four years want this?"*, which she then
   has to answer defensively.
2. It puts her in a pile with final-year students, screened by whoever handles that pile.
3. **It sets the band.** The AlgoUniversity row is this week's evidence: once she is in a
   firm's system at a given level, that level is what she is.

**Three better frames, in order of strength:**

**1. Propose the work, not the slot. This is the strongest one.** A nine-person fund cannot
easily find "an internship", but it can always use a sector view nobody has time to write.
*"I would like to write you a landscape on India's chronic care or diagnostics market over
the next six weeks"* is a proposal someone can say yes to in one line. **"Can I intern with
you" is a request that creates work for the reader.** Offer a deliverable and she is a
contributor from the first message rather than an applicant.

**2. "Fixed term" or "Venture Fellow".** Eight to twelve weeks, defined start and end. It
says the same thing as intern without the student connotation, and the end date is what makes
a partner comfortable saying yes.

**3. Use the firm's own word where they have one.** 3one4 runs a programme it calls an
internship. In that one case, call it an internship, because that is the queue that exists.

**And the learning frame is the ask, not the qualification.** "I want to learn the for profit
side" is a good reason to be writing. It is not the reason they should say yes. The reason
they should say yes is that she has spent four years making funding decisions in Indian
healthcare and can do the research work from day one. **Lead with what she can do, then say
what she wants to learn.** The other order reads as asking for a favour.

**One thing she should not say:** anything that frames philanthropy as not-quite-real
experience. She assessed founders, ran diligence, set evidence bars, and took
recommendations to a body that approved every commitment. The money did not expect a return;
the judgement was real. **"I have been making investment decisions with capital that never
expected a return, and I want to learn how that judgement changes when it does"** is the
honest version, and it is a far better sentence than any apology.

## The messages

All drafts are dash free and hyphen free. **LinkedIn is the default channel**, with email as
the fallback or the second touch.

### Step 1 · the connection note, under 200 characters

**Generic, 186 characters:**

> Hi [name], four years at Tata Trusts deciding which health technologies in India deserved
> funding. Moving to the commercial side of that now and trying to learn it from people who
> do it.

**For Dr Ramesh Byrapaneni at Endiya, 189 characters:**

> Hi Ramesh, four years at Tata Trusts deciding which health technologies in India deserved
> funding. Moving to the commercial side of that now and would value learning from how you
> assess it.

**For Social Alpha, 167 characters** — the only one that names the firm, because the Tata
Trusts link makes that honest rather than flattering:

> Hi [name], I spent four years on the healthcare portfolio at Tata Trusts. Social Alpha is
> the closest thing to what I want to do next, so I wanted to introduce myself.

**For Dilip Kumar at Rainmatter, 179 characters:**

> Hi Dilip, four years at Tata Trusts deciding which health technologies in India deserved
> funding. Moving to the commercial side of that now and keen to learn how Rainmatter picks.

### Step 2 · the message once they accept, or the email body · 265 words

**Subject line for email:** Four years funding health technology, looking to learn the
commercial side

> Hi [name],
>
> I spent the last four years at Tata Trusts looking after a healthcare grant portfolio of
> about ₹450 crore. The work was screening thirty to fifty proposals a month, running
> diligence on the ones that survived, and taking funding recommendations to the CEO and the
> Board of Trustees, which approved every commitment. Health technology and AI diagnostics
> were the areas I owned, so a lot of it was separating what genuinely worked from what had
> worked once in favourable conditions.
>
> So I have spent four years making funding decisions, with money that never expected a
> return. I would like to learn how that judgement changes when it does, and I would rather
> learn it inside a fund than by reading about it.
>
> What I am asking for is narrower than a job. A fixed term seat of eight to twelve weeks, on
> whatever terms work for you, doing the research and diligence work nobody has time for. If
> it helps you decide, I would be glad to start by writing you a landscape of [sector] and
> sending it over, so you can see how I think before anything is agreed.
>
> Before the Trusts I started a consumer business incubated at Ashoka, where I did the market
> sizing and unit economics and pitched it to around twenty funds, so I have also seen this
> from the other side of the table.
>
> I left the Trusts in September, so I can start whenever suits.
>
> Nikita Sachanandani
> +91 7698030306
> sachanandani.nikita@gmail.com

### Why it is built this way

**The ask is narrower than a job, and it is stated as such.** "I am asking for something
narrower than a job" removes the thing that makes a partner hesitate, which is being asked to
create and justify a headcount.

**"Money that never expected a return. I would like to learn how that judgement changes when
it does."** This is the whole pitch in two sentences. It claims the four years honestly,
names the gap honestly, and makes the gap the reason for writing rather than a weakness to be
glossed.

**The offer of a written landscape is the part most likely to produce a reply**, and it should
be filled in per firm rather than left generic: chronic care or diagnostics for Endiya,
maternal and child health or NCDs for Menterra, women's health for Rainmatter, healthtech for
3one4. **Naming their own thesis back to them, and offering to do work inside it, is the
difference between a message about her and a message about them.**

**The Tiff.in paragraph earns its place here**, unusually. She pitched to twenty funds, which
means she has been on the receiving end of exactly the judgement she is asking to learn. One
sentence, no more.

### The 3one4 variant

Same body, two changes. Open with *"I saw you run an internship programme and run it on a
rolling basis, so I am writing to ask whether you would consider someone with four years of
experience rather than a student"*, and **use their word, internship**. Send it to
`internships@3one4capital.com`, and attach the sector view rather than offering it, since
1,500 applications a year means the attachment is the only thing that separates her.

## Before she starts

1. **Send Kae Capital today.** Three drafts, written in August, never sent, best-matched cold
   note in the repo. It belongs at the front of this exercise, not behind eight new firms.
2. **Decide the terms before anyone asks.** Unpaid is a reasonable offer and it raises the
   yes rate, but **set the end date in writing and the scope in writing.** A fixed term
   engagement with no end date becomes free labour, and at four years of experience she can
   afford to be clear rather than grateful. She has no comp floor in this search, so the
   thing to protect is not the money, **it is the end date and the deliverable.**
3. **Write one sector landscape before sending anything.** One document, eight to twelve
   pages, on Indian diagnostics or chronic care, using the geography and cost-effectiveness
   thinking she already does. **It is the attachment for every message on this list**, it
   costs one weekend, and it is the only thing that distinguishes her from the 1,500
   applications 3one4 gets a year.
4. **Expect the hit rate to be low and the volume to be the strategy.** Eight firms, six
   LinkedIn notes and three emails, is a week. This lane has no postings to apply to, so
   outreach is not the fallback, it is the channel.
5. **Do not let this displace the four live routes.** Beyond, Qurist (message and CV ready
   today), the 2070 Health introduction, and Farro at FIT 4.6 can all produce a paid role now.
   **This list produces a seat in three months at best**, and that is worth building toward,
   not worth trading for.
6. **Verify every name and title before sending.** Marked `[VERIFY]` above where sources
   disagree or are old. Endiya's titles came from their own team page and are solid. Ventureast,
   Unitus and Rainmatter titles come from third-party coverage, some of it several years old.
   **Getting someone's title wrong in the first line is the one unforced error available here.**

# The four line version · added 2026-10-09

Her brief: *"super quirky cold outreach email, not more than four lines… founders and managing
partners don't really have the time to read through long emails."* **Correct, and these
replace the 265 word email for a genuine cold approach to a partner.** Keep the long version
only for Social Alpha, where the Tata Trusts link gives her standing to write at length, and
for 3one4, where the programme expects an application.

**On "quirky", since it is easy to get wrong.** To a managing partner, quirk that works is
**one unexpected true detail**, not a joke. The line that does it here is that her job was
mostly *declining* things. Nobody writing to a fund says that, it is immediately memorable,
and it is the most accurate one sentence summary of four years of screening thirty to fifty
proposals a month. **A gag would read as someone trying to be liked; an odd true fact reads as
someone with judgement.**

All three are addressed to Ramesh Byrapaneni at Endiya as the example. Swap the name, the
city in the last line, and the sector.

## A · the "no" angle. **Send this one.** 78 words

**Subject:** Four years of saying no to health startups

> Hi Ramesh, I spent four years at Tata Trusts deciding which health technologies in India got
> funded, which mostly meant saying no to more than a thousand of them.
>
> The part I was good at was telling what worked from what had worked once in favourable
> conditions.
>
> I would like to do that where the money expects a return, and I think I could be useful on
> your diligence while I learn it.
>
> Any chance of a coffee?

**Why this one.** The first line is the quirk and it is entirely true: thirty to fifty
proposals a month over four years is well past a thousand, and almost all of them were
declined. **"I think I could be useful on your diligence while I learn it" is the whole pitch
in one clause** and it gets the order right, usefulness first and learning second. No title,
no ask for a job, no mention of a fellowship, which keeps the reply cheap.

`[CHECK]` "more than a thousand" is the conservative version of 30 to 50 a month across four
years. Defensible under questioning, which is the test.

## B · the no returns angle. 67 words

**Subject:** ₹450 crore, no returns expected

> Hi Ramesh, I ran a ₹450 crore healthcare portfolio at Tata Trusts for four years, with money
> that never expected a return.
>
> I would like to learn the version where it does, from people who do it rather than from
> reading about it.
>
> Happy to earn the seat: pick a sector and I will write you the landscape on it before you
> decide anything.
>
> Worth a chat?

**The subject line is the quirk here**, and it is the best one of the three: a number that
size next to "no returns expected" is a contradiction a partner will want resolved. Line
three is the strongest sentence available to her in any version, because **it moves the burden
from him to her.** Use this one where she wants to signal she will work rather than watch.

## C · the plainest, and the only one that names the ask. 69 words

**Subject:** Could I buy you a coffee

> Hi Ramesh, four years at Tata Trusts choosing which health technologies in India deserved
> funding, so diligence and evidence are the parts I am good at.
>
> I am after a twelve week fellowship with a fund to learn the commercial side of that
> judgement.
>
> I would do the unglamorous research while I am there, and I am free to start now.
>
> Could I buy you a coffee in Hyderabad?

Use this when she wants no ambiguity about what she is asking for. **"Twelve week fellowship"
and "unglamorous research" are the two phrases carrying it**: the first bounds the commitment,
the second says she knows what the work actually is. Slightly less memorable than A and B
because it spends a line on the ask rather than on her.

## Notes for sending

1. **A and B do not name the ask on purpose.** A cold email that asks for nothing except a
   coffee is answerable in four words. The fellowship conversation happens on the call, where
   she can read the room and size it. **C is for when a firm has a programme or when she would
   rather not have the conversation twice.**
2. **Change the last line per city.** Coffee in Hyderabad for Endiya, Ventureast and Anthill.
   Bengaluru for Social Alpha, 3one4, Menterra, Unitus and Rainmatter, where she should say
   she is travelling or offer a call instead, since she is Mumbai based.
3. **Never send the same subject line to two firms at the same fund family or city.** These
   people talk to each other.
4. **No dashes or hyphens anywhere in all three**, consistent with the rest of her outreach.
