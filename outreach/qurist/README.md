### The post says healthcare. The business is consumer wellness with a clinical wrapper.

Worth saying plainly, because the framing in the post is generous. "Build the next chapter
of healthcare in India" describes a D2C brand selling CBD for pain, sleep and anxiety, with
an AYUSH licence and a free-consultation funnel.

**On her board this sits in the same tier as Gynoveda, The Good Bug and Wellbeing
Nutrition** — healthcare-adjacent consumer wellness — rather than with Dozee, Eka Care or
2070 Health. Her domain is health delivery and funding; this is a product business with a
medical claim attached. That is not a reason not to apply. It is a reason not to walk in
expecting her healthcare portfolio to be the qualification.

### And the evidence tension is real, which cuts both ways

CBD for pain, anxiety and sleep is an evidence-light category. She has spent four years
setting deliberately heavy evidence bars and deciding which health technologies worked
versus which worked once in favourable conditions.

**That is either the most valuable thing she could bring to this company or a source of
friction with it, and she should find out which on the first call.** A founder who is
ex-Deloitte and sells under an AYUSH licence has thought about substantiation; whether he
wants someone pushing on it is the open question.

### Why the write up is built from questions

**Because she has not used the products and cannot see the numbers**, and a write up that
pronounced on their strategy from outside would be easy to dismiss. Four questions, each
tied to something she has actually done, show the same judgement without claiming knowledge
she does not have. Point 3 is the strongest of the four, because "six years in without
outside capital means someone already knows this" is a real observation about their
situation rather than a generic one.

**Point 4 names the evidence tension rather than hiding it.** If Samisht wants someone
pushing on substantiation, that paragraph is the reason he calls. If he does not, she has
found out early, which is worth more than a polite interview. It is phrased as a question
about usefulness, not as a criticism of their claims, which is the line that matters.

### Two things to add before sending

1. **Order a product first and add one sentence of first-hand observation.** At ₹1,359 to
   ₹2,549 it is the cheapest possible upgrade to this email, and it is the one thing none of
   the other applicants will have bothered to do. One honest line about the buying
   experience, the packaging or the consultation would sit well at the end of the intro
   paragraph.
2. **Decide on availability.** Nothing in the email mentions that she has left the Trusts,
   consistent with how the Beyond email was handled. **But a seventeen-person company hiring
   a chief of staff wants someone soon**, and she can start immediately, which is a genuine
   advantage here. If she wants it in, one line before the sign off does it: *"I am free to
   start straight away, so timing is not a constraint on my side."*

### Sequencing

**Send the DM today. Hold this email for 48 hours.** If Samisht replies, this may not be
needed at all, or it becomes the follow up he asks for. If he does not reply, send it

Do not send both inside the same hour. At a company this size Samisht and Sarika sit in the
same room, and two messages arriving together reads as scattered rather than keen.

## Before she writes

1. **Location is the open gate and it is not in the post.** No city is stated anywhere, and
   the company's registered base was not established from the public pages. **Ask in the
   DM or the first reply.** Remote, Mumbai, Bangalore and Delhi NCR are fine; anything else
   is not, and it would be a waste to get three conversations deep before finding out.
2. **Comp will be constrained.** Unfunded, under ₹10 crore, seventeen people. A chief of
   staff seat there is unlikely to match her previous fixed. Decide the floor in advance.
3. **Ask how the business is funded and what the plan is.** Not confrontationally, but it
   is the central question. Bootstrapped and profitable is a genuinely good place to be a
   chief of staff. Bootstrapped and stuck is not, and the difference decides whether this
   role leads anywhere.
4. **She is immediately available**, which at a seventeen-person company that wants someone
   now is worth saying.
5. **Do not lead with the ₹450 crore.** To a founder running a sub-₹10 crore business, a
   portfolio number that size invites the thought that she will find the work small. Lead
   with evidence and judgement, which is what the DM does.
