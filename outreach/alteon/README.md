## What they are

Bengaluru deep-tech aerospace company. **Founded 2025 by Samay Sanghvi, who is 20**, and
who started on it out of high school in 2023 by building and crashing radio-controlled
models.

They build small fixed-wing autonomous aircraft that stay airborne by **dynamic soaring**,
an albatross-inspired technique that extracts energy from wind shear over the ocean by
repeatedly climbing into faster air and dropping back toward the surface. The goal is more
than a year aloft with no fuel or propulsion battery. First design is roughly a three metre
wingspan.

## Why the timing is unusually good

They raised **one week ago**, have **20 people and no commercial function**, and every
application on their list runs through Indian government and public institutions: Coast
Guard, Navy, fisheries departments, disaster management, the met department.

A twenty-person all-technical team that has just raised and is heading toward government
customers has a gap it has probably not named yet. That gap is the one thing Nikita has
genuinely done.

## The honest problems

- **The money is earmarked for engineers.** Coverage says the raise funds CFD and
  aeronautical engineering teams, flight-control R&D and field testing. A non-technical
  commercial hire may simply not be on the plan yet.
- **Pre-seed comp at 20 people** will sit well below her band.
- **She has no aviation domain**, and should not pretend otherwise for one second.
- **Interest is not a job.** She has said plainly that she has no strategic reason to pivot
  beyond finding the industry interesting. That is fine as a motive and useless as a pitch,
  which is why the message below leads with something concrete instead.

## Why honesty is the right play here

Nikita's instinct to say "I just find this interesting" is worth keeping rather than
dressing up. Founders are pitched constantly by people manufacturing strategic rationales,
and a 20 year old founder a week after his first raise will be getting a great deal of it.

A short note that says *I find this genuinely interesting, here is the one thing I could
actually help with, and I am not asking you for a job* is disarming precisely because
almost nobody sends it. The credibility comes from the specific thing, not from the
enthusiasm.

## Before she sends

1. **Watch the TechCrunch piece and the Bay of Bengal footage first.** If he replies within
   the hour, she needs to be able to hold a conversation about dynamic soaring for five
   minutes. The concept is understandable without any aeronautics.
2. **Do not claim aviation knowledge.** In a company of engineers, an overclaim is found out
   immediately, and the honest position is stronger anyway.
3. **Do not send a CV unless he asks.** The message is the application.
4. **Expect nothing, or expect a conversation.** He is 20, a week past a raise, and hiring
   engineers. The realistic outcome is a relationship that matters in a year, not a role
   this month. That is still worth twenty minutes.
