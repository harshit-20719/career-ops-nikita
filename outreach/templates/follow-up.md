# Follow-up

For silence. One follow-up, then stop — the second one doesn't convert and it's the one
they remember.

Two shapes depending on what you're chasing.

---

## After an application, no response (7–10 days)

**Subject:** Following up — <Role> application

> Hi <Name>,
>
> I applied for the <Role> role on <date> and wanted to follow up briefly.
>
> <One sentence adding something rather than repeating the resume: a relevant thing
> you've shipped, a piece of their product you've been using, why the fit still looks
> right to you.>
>
> Happy to send anything else that would help. Is the role still open?
>
> <Name>

The closing question matters — it gives them a one-word reply, which is far easier than
composing a status update, and either answer is useful to you.

---

## After a stated timeline passes (2–3 days past)

**Subject:** Checking in — <Role>

> Hi <Name>,
>
> Thanks again for the conversation on <date>. You'd mentioned hearing back around
> <date they said> — I wanted to check whether there's an update, and whether you need
> anything further from me.
>
> Still very interested in <specific thing about the role>.
>
> <Name>

Referencing their own timeline back to them is the point. It's a factual nudge, not a
complaint, and hiring processes slip constantly without anyone remembering to say so.

---

## Notes

- Never sound aggrieved. Slipped timelines are almost always internal chaos, not a
  verdict on you.
- Reply in the existing thread when there is one — it carries the context.
- Add one new thing. A follow-up that only says "just checking in" gives them nothing
  to respond to.
- Mark the row `ghosted` at 21 days after your last contact and move on. That number
  isn't pessimism; it's what keeps the pipeline count honest.
