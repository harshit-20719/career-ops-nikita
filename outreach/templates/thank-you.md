# Thank-you note

Within 24 hours of every round, to everyone you spoke with individually. It rarely wins
you the job on its own, but a specific one keeps you memorable through a week of
deliberation — and skipping it is noticed at some companies.

Write it straight from the debrief, while the details are still sharp.

---

**Subject:** Thanks for today / Thank you — <Role>

> Hi <Name>,
>
> Thanks for taking the time today. <One specific thing from the conversation that
> genuinely interested you — the migration they described, the way the team splits
> ownership, the problem they said was hardest.>
>
> <One sentence connecting it to something you've done or want to do. This is where you
> reinforce fit without restating the resume.>
>
> <Optional: a better version of an answer you fumbled — one or two sentences, no
> apology attached.>
>
> Looking forward to hearing about next steps.
>
> <Name>

---

## Notes

- **Individual notes, different content.** If three people compare identical emails, it
  reads worse than sending nothing. The debrief gives you a distinct detail per person.
- **The specific detail is the entire value.** A generic thank-you is filler.
- **The recovery line is legitimate** — a cleaner answer to a question you flubbed,
  offered briefly and confidently. Don't apologize for the original answer; just give
  the better one.
- **Short.** Five sentences. They're busy and still interviewing other people.
- No new asks. The next step is theirs, and this note isn't a follow-up.
- No email address? Ask the recruiter for it, or send it to the recruiter and ask them
  to pass it on.
