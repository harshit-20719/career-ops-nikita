# Cold outreach

Someone you've never met, at a company you want. Reply rates are low by nature — the
specific detail in the second line is what lifts them above noise.

---

**Subject:** Question about <specific thing>

> Hi <Name>,
>
> I'm <one clause about yourself — role and where>. <One sentence naming something
> specific of theirs: a launch, a post, a talk, a design decision — and one concrete
> reaction to it that shows you actually engaged.>
>
> I'm looking at <role type> roles and <Company> is high on my list because <the real
> reason, in one clause>. Would you have 15 minutes in the next couple of weeks to tell
> me what working on <team/product> is actually like?
>
> Completely understand if the timing doesn't work.
>
> <Name>

---

## Notes

- **The second sentence carries the message.** If you can't write something specific
  about their work, you're not ready to send this — go read for twenty minutes.
- Ask about their experience, not for a job. The referral, if it comes, comes after.
- Name the time commitment. "15 minutes" is a decision someone can make in two seconds.
- The out at the end ("understand if...") measurably helps. It removes the obligation
  and makes replying feel voluntary.
- No attachments. A resume in a cold email is presumptuous and often filtered.

## LinkedIn variant

Same content, harder limit — 300 characters on a connection request. Cut to: who you
are, the specific detail, the ask. Send the full version as a message once they accept.

> Hi <Name> — <one clause about yourself>. Really liked <specific thing>. I'm exploring
> <role type> roles and would love 15 min on what <team> is like. No worries if not.
