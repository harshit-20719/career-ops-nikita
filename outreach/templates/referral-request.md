# Referral request

Someone you actually know, for a specific open role. This is the single
highest-conversion move in a job search — referred candidates get read.

Make it trivially easy to say yes: name the role, attach the resume, and hand them
language they can paste into the internal form.

---

**Subject:** Referral for the <Role> role at <Company>?

> Hi <Name>,
>
> <One warm line that isn't filler — something real from the last time you spoke.>
>
> <Company> has an opening for <Role> (<link>) and I'm planning to apply. Given <the
> honest reason you're a fit — a project, a shared past problem, relevant scope>, I
> thought it was worth asking whether you'd be comfortable referring me.
>
> Resume attached. If it helps, here's something you could paste:
>
> > <Two or three sentences in their voice: how you know each other, what you did
> > together, why you'd be good here. Written so they can send it without editing.>
>
> No pressure at all if you'd rather not, or don't know my work well enough — I'd
> genuinely rather ask than assume.
>
> <Name>

---

## Notes

- **Write the blurb for them.** Referral forms ask "how do you know this person and why
  should we hire them", and having to compose that is the actual friction. Removing it
  is most of why this works.
- **Attach the tailored resume**, not the master. Here the attachment is expected.
- **Give a real out.** Someone who barely knows your work should be able to decline
  without it being awkward — and preserving that relationship is worth more than one
  referral.
- **Apply anyway** if they don't reply within a week. Don't let a pending referral hold
  the application hostage.
- **Report back either way.** Tell them when you get the interview, and tell them when
  you get rejected. This is why people refer the same person twice.
