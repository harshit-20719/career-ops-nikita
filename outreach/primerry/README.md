### Dr Nihar Parekh is the more interesting half of the founding pair

MBBS from MGM Medical College, Navi Mumbai (2000), D.Ch. and M.D. in Paediatrics.
**Co-founder and Director of Cheers Child Care**, which he established in 2008 with his
father, Dr Pankaj Parekh — a Mumbai practice combining preventive paediatric and
sick-child care under one roof. Seventeen years in practice. He writes for KidsStopPress.

His stated mission, in third-party profiles, is **taking paediatrics "towards an
evidence-based medicine practice."** And his quote on the raise:

> "Over years of pediatric practice, we have seen a clear gap in how everyday health needs,
> such as growth, immunity, gut health, and coughs and colds are managed. We are building
> clean formulations with unique delivery formats."

**Remember that phrase — evidence-based — because it is the hook.**

## The angle

**The offline channel is the hardest part of their plan, and it is the one place her
background is genuinely unusual.**

They have said offline runs through **pharmacies, paediatric clinics and mother-and-baby
speciality stores.** Of those three, paediatric clinics are the hardest to open and the
most defensible once open — and opening institutional healthcare relationships from a
standing start is literally what she did fifteen times at the Trusts, with hospital
systems among them.

**The combination is the pitch, not either half.** Institutional healthcare relationships
and consumer-brand building rarely sit in the same person. Their cap table is full of D2C
operators and their clinical credibility comes from one practising doctor. The gap between
those two is exactly where she sits.

**The second angle, if the first does not land:** they are differentiating on *clinical
credibility* rather than marketing, and Dr Parekh's stated mission is evidence-based
paediatrics. She spent four years setting a deliberately heavier evidence bar on health
technologies — evaluating across varied conditions rather than favourable ones, and
choosing settings that stripped out confounders — specifically to tell the difference
between something that works and something that worked once. A brand selling on clinical
credibility to sceptical parents has that problem in commercial form.

### Who to write to

**Rushabh Nandu first.** He is the business co-founder, the "RN" in RN Kids, and the one
quoted on parents wanting "clean, non-medicated solutions" and on bringing global
formulations and sourcing standards. He owns the commercial build, which is where the
proposed role sits.

**Dr Nihar Parekh second**, on the evidence angle. He is more publicly reachable — the
clinic, the KidsStopPress column — but he owns clinical credibility rather than hiring.

## Before she writes

1. **Check the name.** `primerry.in`, not `primerry.com`. Writing to the art-class company
   would be the worst possible opening.
2. **Sign up for early access on primerry.in.** It is one field, it is what a prospective
   customer would do, and it means she can say something true about their launch funnel.
3. **Read one Cheers Child Care page.** Dr Parekh's practice is the origin of the product
   thesis, and if either founder replies it will come up.
4. **Do not lead with the ₹450 crore portfolio.** Same mistake as Fragaria would have
   been. To two founders shipping eight SKUs, a philanthropic portfolio number is
   impressive and irrelevant. Lead with the channel and the brand.
5. **Do not quote the cap table attributions.** The Moms Co. and The Man Company
   connections are reported inconsistently across sources. Knowing they are consumer
   operators is enough to shape the note; naming them risks being wrong about a
   stranger's own investors.
6. **Decide her comp floor before the first call, not during it.** ₹7.1 crore pre-seed is
   roughly $760,000. That is a small team with maybe eighteen months of runway, and the
   offer will very likely sit below her current fixed. Her numbers stay in `private/`.
7. **Ask what the founding-team roles actually are.** "Build out its founding team" is the
   only hiring signal that exists, and it could mean a brand lead, a supply-chain lead, or
   a generalist first business hire. The third is the one worth having.

## What to go in knowing

- **Pre-launch means zero commercial evidence.** No revenue, no retention, no proof the
  eight SKUs sell. The whole thesis is a doctor's clinical observation plus a consumer
  team's conviction.
- **₹7.1 crore is small.** Comp will likely be below her current fixed, and the realistic
  title is a first-business-hire seat rather than a senior one.
- **This is the sixth role this search where her healthcare domain is only partly load
  bearing** — but unlike Cube, Honasa, Incresco, Lumiere and Fragaria, it is at least
  *in* healthcare. That is a meaningful improvement on the last five.
- **And it is ungated.** No degree line, no years band, no stated credential, because
  there is no posting. After nineteen roles killed on a credential screen, that is the
  category that has actually worked for her.
