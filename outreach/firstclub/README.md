## What the CV is built to answer

A Series B quick-commerce founder's office is a **geography and unit-economics job**: which
cities, which dark stores, which categories, at what contribution margin, and which
partnerships unlock them. So the CV is swapped to lead with that spine rather than with
sector.

**The hero bullet is the heat map, and it is genuinely well-matched here:**

> Built a geospatial investment heat map across Indian states, scoring and comparing
> returns by geography and sub-theme — analyzing how **an identical model produced
> materially different returns across regions and terrains**; findings directly shaped
> capital allocation from 2022–2024, identifying where to concentrate, **where local
> conditions changed the economics**, and what returns to expect by region.

Quick commerce is the same problem in commercial clothing. The same store format, the same
basket, the same promise — and wildly different economics by pincode. She has built the
model that answers that question and had it change allocation for two years. **That is the
single most transferable thing she owns for this company**, and it has nothing to do with
healthcare.

### The other swaps, and why

- **"grant partnerships" → "the portfolio"; "deal lifecycle" → "partnership lifecycle".**
  The philanthropic framing is removed wherever it can go honestly, because it is noise to
  this reader.
- **"ensured every rupee tracked against impact targets" → "unit-cost modelling".** Backed
  by the cost-per-beneficiary modelling in the master, and it is the language this company
  thinks in.
- **"proactive grantmaking targets" → "proactive origination targets"**, and
  **"cost-per-beneficiary efficiency" → "unit-cost efficiency"**.
- **"portfolio dashboards" → "performance dashboards"**, presented to the CEO.
- **LetsEndorse** now reads "synthesized demand and market data across 22 Tier III & IV
  cities into recommendations on **where to locate projects**" — city-level location
  analysis across 22 cities is directly relevant to a company expanding city by city, and
  it was buried before.
- **Tiff.in keeps its one-line descriptor** and nothing more, per her instruction. For a
  consumer company it earns its place; it is still a one-year stint.
- **"healthcare" stays in the portfolio bullet.** It is true, it is her depth, and removing
  it would be both dishonest and pointless — the reader will see Tata Trusts regardless.
  It is simply no longer the headline.

### Where the inference stops

Without a JD I cannot know whether this founder's office is analytics-led, ops-led, or
fundraise-led. **If a JD or a conversation surfaces, the CV should be re-cut.** The version
built here is the analytics-and-expansion reading, which is the most likely shape at Series
B and the one that plays to her strengths.

## The honest assessment

**Not scored, because there is no role.** If there were, FIT would land around 3.0–3.3 and
the reasons are worth stating plainly:

**For her:**
- Bengaluru, P0. For-profit. India-market. All gates clear.
- Well funded and fast — $83M, Series B eighteen months in, tripled valuation in three
  months. This is not a company that runs out of money next year.
- The geography-and-returns model, the unit-economics work, 15+ partnerships originated
  and closed, and CEO/Board-level reporting are all real and all relevant.
- A founder who has been a VP at Flipkart and CEO of Cleartrip will read a CV for judgement
  rather than for pedigree.

**Against her, and these are not small:**
- **No retail, no e-commerce, no supply chain, no P&L ownership.** A company built by
  ex-Flipkart and ex-Myntra operators has a deep bench of people who have actually run
  quick commerce. She would be the least experienced person in the room on the core
  business.
- **Not healthcare.** This would be the **seventh** role this search where she spends her
  sharpest differentiator, after Cube, Honasa, Incresco, Lumiere, Fragaria and Primerry.
- **No posted role and no referral route found.** Pure cold outreach into a well-funded
  company that is not advertising the seat.
- **Quick commerce is brutal.** Margins are thin, the category is crowded, and "premium"
  is an untested position in Indian q-commerce. That is a business risk, not a reason not
  to apply, but it belongs in her thinking.

**What to do with it:** send the CV if she has a route in. **Do not build the week around
it.** It is a speculative approach to a company with no open seat, and the scan on
2026-10-06 turned up two healthcare roles in Bengaluru that are posted, open, and better
matched — see `applications/evals/2026-10-06-scan-blr-hyd.md`.

If she does approach, **Ayyappan Rajagopal is the only sensible target**, and the opening
should be the geography point rather than anything about herself: a question about how
they are choosing which cities and categories come next, and what changes the economics
between them.
