## The angle, and why it is not the obvious one

The obvious pitch is the ₹450 crore portfolio and four years of evaluating what to fund.
**That is the wrong opener here** — it is a healthcare portfolio at a philanthropic
institution, and to a seed-stage farming founder it reads as impressive and irrelevant.

The right opener is the part of her CV nobody has used yet.

### What she actually has that Fragaria does not

**1. NABARD. This is the real credential and it has been sitting unused.**

At NabFoundation (NABARD) she **assessed the financial feasibility of $240M of projects
across 12+ states** through field visits, budgetary analysis and impact framework
research, worked with the Government of India in the North-East, and supported
collaborations with SBI, NatWest and Ampersand. NABARD is *the* agricultural and rural
development bank in India.

Three founders from India, Mexico and South Africa with agronomy, consulting and
operating backgrounds plausibly do not know the Indian agricultural-finance and
state-incentive landscape. **She does, and it is checkable.**

**Caveat she must respect: this was a two-month internship in early 2021.** It is a real
credential and a genuine conversation starter. It is not four years of agri-finance
experience, and overclaiming it would be the same mistake as the Tiff.in description.

**2. The state-by-state returns model, which maps directly onto their live decision.**

She built a geospatial investment heat map across Indian states, **scoring and comparing
returns by geography and sub-theme**, which showed identical interventions producing
materially different results by region and terrain, and it redirected allocation for two
years.

Facility two's location is undecided. **Where they build changes their incentive package
as much as their cost base.** That is the same class of question her strongest piece of
work answered.

**3. Government-scheme sequencing, which is a timing point rather than a money point.**

Protected cultivation in India is supported under **MIDH**, administered through the
**National Horticulture Board** for commercial projects above 4,000 sq m, at up to 50% of
project cost — **but capped at roughly ₹56 lakh per project**, and **work must begin only
after NHB approval**.

> **Be precise about this in conversation, because the honest version is more useful than
> the exciting one.** Against a $3.5–4M build, a ₹56 lakh cap is about 1.6% of cost. The
> central capital subsidy is close to noise at their scale, and claiming otherwise would
> be caught instantly by a CA.
>
> **The non-obvious part is the sequencing and the state layer.** Approval has to precede
> construction, and facility two is slated for early 2027 — so if any of it is worth
> having, the decision point is now. And state-level horticulture incentives vary
> materially between states, which matters precisely because the siting decision is open.
>
> `[VERIFY]` Scheme rates and company eligibility change year to year. She should frame
> all of this **as a question, not a claim.** A question from someone who clearly knows
> the landscape exists is credible. A confident number that turns out stale destroys the
> note.

**4. Oh! Fruits is a consumer brand, and she has built one.**

Tiff.in Productions: incubated at Ashoka as one of three teams funded out of thirty-five,
**520% growth in YouTube views and 370% in Instagram followers** across four channels,
plus the market sizing, unit economics and investor-ready projections, and pitches to 20+
VC funds. For a premium D2C fruit brand fighting three-months-a-year seasonality, that is
on point in a way the Trusts work is not.

## Before she sends

1. **Lead with NABARD and the state model. Do not lead with the ₹450 crore portfolio.**
   To a seed-stage farming founder the portfolio is impressive and irrelevant, and leading
   with it signals she has not thought about what he needs.
2. **Frame the scheme point as a question.** Rates and corporate eligibility move
   year to year, and a CA will check. "What did you find when you looked" is strong;
   a specific percentage that turns out stale is fatal.
3. **Do not oversell NABARD.** Two months, early 2021. Say "two months" if asked, or say
   it unprompted — it costs almost nothing and protects the rest of the note.
4. **Read one Oh! Fruits product page before writing.** If he replies, the first thing he
   will want to know is whether she has actually looked at the brand.
5. **Ask which city the role would be in.** Bengaluru is P0 for her; **Chennai is not on
   her list at all**, and the registered office is in Chennai while operations are moving
   to Bengaluru. This needs settling before any conversation gets serious, not after.
6. **Expect this to be a small, unfunded seat if it exists at all.** Three founders,
   $2M raised, no careers page. The realistic shapes are founder's office, business or
   commercial lead, or fundraising support for the facility-two round. A senior title is
   not on the table and she should not open by asking for one.
7. **If Harish does not reply in ~10 days**, send E, then try Timothy on the brand angle.
   WEH Ventures' Rohit Krishna is a third route via the investor side, but go there last —
   approaching a founder through their investor without reason reads badly.
