# Anu Duggal, Female Founders Fund — connection note

Drafted 2026-10-08. **This is a relationship note, not an application**, and the drafts are
written that way on purpose.

**Why that distinction matters:** Female Founders Fund is New York based, invests in US
women-led technology companies (Zola, Eloquii, Billie, Tala), and **no Indian portfolio
companies were found**. At roughly $100M AUM across its funds it is a small team. A note
here will not produce a role, and writing one that angles for a role would be the fastest
way to get no reply.

What it can produce is a genuinely useful person to know: an India-origin operator who
built in India, moved to allocating capital, and now runs a fund in a sector Nikita knows.
That is worth having on its own terms.

---

## Why she is worth writing to, and the thing not to say

Her path, which is the reason the note will land:

- **Opened India's first wine bar, The Tasting Room, in Mumbai in 2005**, after Vassar and
  cooking school in France
- Co-founded **Exclusively.in**, a flash-sale site widening the reach of Indian artisans,
  **backed by Accel and Tiger Global, acquired by Myntra in 2011**. The business wound down
  in 2012
- **That ending became the catalyst for Female Founders Fund**, launched 2014 with Sutian
  Dong

So: she built a consumer business in India, it ended, and that redirected her into
allocating capital. **Nikita co-founded a consumer business in India, left it inside a
year, spent four years allocating philanthropic capital, and is now trying to move to
commercial capital.** The shape is close enough to be the real reason a note gets opened.

> **⚠ Do not say any of that out loud in the first message.** This is the same discipline
> as the Kae Capital brief: *"'You did this too, help me do it' is presumptuous from a
> stranger and makes the note about her career rather than about the work."* It applies more
> here, not less, because Anu Duggal is a public figure with heavy inbound.
>
> **Her path is why the note works. It is not the content of the note.** She will make the
> connection herself the second she looks at the profile, and she will think better of
> Nikita for not having pointed at it.

## If she accepts, the second message

The connection note opens the door. This is where the substance goes, and it should still
not ask for a job.

> Thanks for connecting.
>
> The short version of me: four years at Tata Trusts managing a $51 million healthcare
> portfolio, which mostly meant screening thirty to fifty proposals a month, running
> diligence on what survived, and taking recommendations to the CEO and the Board. Health
> technology and AI diagnostics were the areas I owned, so a lot of it was deciding which
> health technologies were genuinely ready to deploy in India and which had worked once in
> favourable conditions.
>
> I am moving to the commercial side of that work now, which in India means a fairly small
> number of funds and a lot of cold conversations.
>
> What I would actually value, if you have fifteen minutes at some point: how you think about
> evaluating digital health when the clinical evidence is thin and the company is early. That
> is the judgement I have been making with grant money for four years and I would like to
> know how it differs when the capital expects a return.
>
> No expectations either way, and thank you for reading this far.

**Why that question and not another:** it is a real question she cannot answer herself, it
is specific to what Anu does, and it treats her as a practitioner rather than as a door.
Asking a good question is the only cold outreach that reliably works, and "how does this
judgement change when the capital expects a return" is the exact seam between Nikita's
experience and Anu's.

## Before she sends

1. **Set the expectation internally.** This is a conversation, not a pipeline. **It should
   not displace the four things with live routes**: Beyond, Qurist, 2070 Health and Farro
   Capital, all of which can produce an actual role and three of which are still unsent.
2. **Do not ask about jobs at FFF.** There is no India presence and she would need a US
   visa. Raising it converts a good contact into a declined request.
3. **Verify the Exclusively.in details before referencing them in any later conversation.**
   Accel, Tiger Global and the Myntra acquisition come from third-party profiles, not from
   Anu. Fine as background, risky as a quoted fact.
4. **If she wants the warmer version of this lane**, the research worth doing is
   female-led or female-founded India healthcare and impact funds. Those clear her gates,
   which FFF does not.
