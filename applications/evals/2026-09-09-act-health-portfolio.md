# The eight companies — ACT For Health's portfolio

Researched 2026-09-09.

**All eight are ACT For Health portfolio companies.** Confirmed individually against ACT's
own announcements. The list is her employer-side view of ACT.

## What each one is

| Company | What it does | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Atom360** | AI oral cancer screening. Berry.care analyses oral cavity images from a smartphone app, operable by community health workers with minimal training, aimed at hard-to-reach areas | Patented. Also an XPRIZE team |
| **Bioscan Research** | Cerebo, an ML-powered near-infrared device detecting intracranial bleeds in traumatic brain injury within about two minutes | Mumbai. **$1M seed led by Unicorn India Ventures**. Founded 2017 |
| **C3 Med-Tech** | Portable, affordable AI-enabled eye screening to reduce avoidable blindness | Ahmedabad. **₹2 crore first equity round** |
| **Intelehealth** | Open-source telemedicine platform for rural primary care. **Deployed with the Odisha state government across 30 districts, training ASHAs and ANMs** | **Non-profit** — already on her own `KNOWN_NONPROFIT` list in `scan.py` |
| **Navya Care** | Cancer second opinions. Non-clinical staff map patient data to NCG and NCCN guidelines for fast, low-cost expert review. **80,000+ patients** | Bengaluru, since 2012 |
| **Dvara Health Finance** | Health savings accounts so households don't fall into poverty from medical expenses, bundled with GP access and insurance | Part of the Dvara group |
| **Evolve** | Digital mental health for the LGBTQIA+ community, addressing minority stress through content, community and queer-affirmative coaching | Expanding to 100,000+ users via NGO, CSR and corporate partnerships |
| **Adagio VR** | VR for mental health, performance and behaviour change | **Primarily UK-based**, operating in India through partnerships. Bootstrapped. ACT onboarded them with Mitr Trust for the LGBTQI+ community |

Others in the same portfolio, not on her list: Niramai, Saathealth, Medoplus, KHPT.

## As application targets: effectively empty

**One live posting across all eight**, and it is a Tech Lead / full-stack developer role at
Intelehealth in Hyderabad. Nothing else, going back ninety days.

That is what a seed-stage health-tech portfolio looks like. These companies are small,
their hiring is overwhelmingly engineering and clinical, and their commercial functions are
usually one or two people who are already there. Comp at this stage would also sit well
below her band.

**Do not spend the week applying into this list.** There is nothing to apply to.

## What the list is actually worth, which is a lot

**Read the portfolio as a specification of what ACT looks for, and Nikita is unusually
well-qualified to assess exactly these bets.**

Look at the pattern. Every single company is a technology that must:

1. **Prove it works clinically at low cost** — Atom360's accuracy from a phone camera,
   Bioscan's detection rate, C3's screening validity, Navya's guideline mapping
2. **Work in a low-resource or last-mile setting** — hard-to-reach areas, rural primary
   care, community health workers
3. **Get deployed through public or institutional systems** — Intelehealth through the
   Odisha government across 30 districts, training ASHAs and ANMs

That is a three-line description of Nikita's job. She has spent four years deciding which
health technologies were ready to deploy and which merely demoed well, structuring pilots
under a deliberately heavier evidence bar, and building programmes *through* government
systems rather than around them. Health-tech and AI diagnostics are sub-themes she owns
outright.

**She would be a strong diligence hire for ACT specifically because of this portfolio, not
despite it.** Atom360 and C3 are screening technologies in low-resource settings, which is
the exact category where she has already formed and defended views with real money behind
them. Intelehealth's Odisha deployment is the government-systems work she does.

### What this changes about the ACT approach

The previous note said to open ACT's careers page and, failing that, write to
`core-pm@actgrants.in`. **This portfolio is what makes that note specific rather than
generic.** She can name the category, not just the organisation: screening and diagnostic
technologies that have to clear an evidence bar and then survive contact with a public
health system, which is the thing she has been paid to judge.

That is a far better opening than "I admire your work."

## Two honest caveats

**Intelehealth is a non-profit**, and it is on her own known-non-profit list. Worth knowing
that ACT's portfolio mixes for-profit startups with non-profits, because it tells her
something about what ACT is: a grant-maker using venture method, not a fund taking equity.
That reinforces the transition-value cost already recorded — ACT would not credential her
out of philanthropy.

**Adagio VR is primarily a UK company** operating in India through partnerships. If she was
looking at it as an employer, that is a different proposition from the Indian startups on
the list.
