## 1. LEGO — Program Manager, Family Impact Portfolio

**FIT 1. Location gate. Nothing else needs checking.**

**Billund, Denmark. Hybrid, roughly 3 days a week on-site.** Job ID 0000038331, Manager
level, Communications / Corporate Affairs & Sustainability.

Billund is not Remote, Mumbai, Bangalore, Hyderabad or Delhi NCR, and three days a week
on-site is not a remote role with a flexible office. A Danish seat also needs work
authorisation, which is the same wall that closed Paul Angell (Chicago) and Rock Health
(US hubs).

Worth noting only so the pattern is on the record: this is the **third** role brought to
me whose disqualifier is that the seat is in another country. The substance was never the
problem in any of the three. If roles like this keep surfacing, the filter to apply before
sending them is one line in the posting — the city.

One thing that *is* interesting about it, and transfers: LEGO states no years requirement
and no degree requirement, asking instead for delivery of "family, social impact, or early
childhood programs from design through", human-centred design, and partnerships with
implementation partners. That is a posting written to describe a job rather than filter a
queue — the exact shape that has worked for her. The shape is right and the geography is
wrong.

Ashoka runs its careers portal on Darwinbox, and that is the **third time** this platform
has blocked automated reading. It is an Angular application behind a Cloudflare Turnstile

### The preliminary read, which you should weigh but not treat as final

**On the title and the centre alone, this fails the sector gate**, and the rubric names
this case specifically. From `RUBRIC.md`, on what stays out of the healthtech carve-out:

> General non-profit work, grant-making, foundation programme roles, and **policy or
> advocacy posts — including in healthcare.**

A Programme Lead at a university public policy centre is a policy post, and it is not
healthcare. More bluntly: programme design, stakeholder management, policy briefs and
institutional partnerships inside a mission-driven institution **is the job you are
leaving**, in a university wrapper rather than a philanthropic one. The title is also a
lateral, not a step up.

### What would change my answer

Three things, and the JD would settle all of them in a minute:

1. **It is actually a health-policy mandate with a technology or AI build** — not policy
   research about health, but owning the thing. That could clear the carve-out.
2. **It is a build-from-zero seat** — the centre or the function does not exist yet, she
   would own standing it up, and there is a named principal whose orbit is itself a
   credential. That is the second door in the rubric, and it is how the Bansal role got
   scored at 4.0/4.0.
3. **The scope is far larger than "Programme Lead" suggests** — centre-level P&L,
   fundraising, a team.

### The one genuine advantage here, whatever the JD says

**Ashoka is her own institution.** The Young India Fellowship and the P.G.D. in Liberal
Studies are hers, which means an internal referral route exists that exists nowhere else
on this board, and the YIF network is a recognised signal in exactly this hiring market.
If the JD turns out to clear the gate, the odds on this would be unusually good. That is
worth five minutes of her time to check rather than dismissing on the title.

**Action: paste the JD, or send the posting's plain text.** I will score it the same day.

## 3. Health Progress Hub — Research & Scaling Officer

This is the one worth real attention, and the rubric gets it wrong, so I want to be
careful about how I say that.

### What it is

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Organisation | Health Progress Hub. **A team of three, working about 1.5 full-time equivalents** |
| What they do | Help high-impact global health charities decide **which countries to launch and scale in**, recruit for hard-to-fill roles, and run applied research on neglected health problems |
| Partners | Fortify Health, Taimaka, LEEP, HealthLearn, Lead-Acid Battery Recycling Initiative, Radius Institute for Road Safety, Clear Solutions, First Embrace, Ambitious Impact |
| Shape | **Contractor. 8 to 40 hours a week, agreed at offer.** Initial six-month contract, strong chance of extension |
| **Location** | **Remote, any time zone** |
| Pay | **$16–25/hour** with relevant experience; current team is at $21 |
| Reports to | Director |
| Deadline | **15 October 2026** — ten days. They review as applications arrive and may offer before the deadline |
| Process | Form (30–60 min) → **paid** 2.5-hour test task → interview → live work test → interview → references |
| Speed | *"We review every application within 48 hours and may make an offer within a week"* |

### The gate fails. Saying so first, so the rubric stays intact.

**Sector gate: FAIL. FIT 1 as a job.**

Against the healthtech/AI carve-out's three tests:

1. **Healthcare is the domain** — yes, exclusively. ✅
2. **Healthtech or AI is the substance of the job** — no. "Transformative AI and global
   health" is one of their four applied research areas, but the job is prioritisation
   research and research management. ✗
3. **Strong on its own terms** — no. The title is **"Officer"**, the same noun she is
   trying to leave behind, on a six-month contractor agreement. ✗

Against the build-from-zero exception:

1. **The function does not exist yet** — genuinely true. *"This is our first hire"*, and
   *"To date, our applied research has been limited and mostly internal."* ✅
2. **She would own the build** — largely true. *"Improve the methods and quality
   standards HPH uses"*, *"Develop HPH's research agenda"*, *"What you build here shapes
   how we work, whom we hire next."* ✅
3. **The principal is a genuine credential** — no. Three people, ~1.5 FTE, no named
   principal whose orbit is itself worth being in. The *network* around the work is
   strong (CHAI mentors, senior African government officials, founders of GiveWell-backed
   charities, Ambitious Impact), but a network is not a principal. ✗

So both doors close, and by the rubric this is a 1.

### And now the part the rubric cannot see

**The rubric scores jobs. This is not a job.**

**8 to 40 hours a week.** *"We welcome applications from people with other ongoing
commitments."* *"We also work part-time with people building their own projects."* *"We
may offer part-time contract work before a final decision."*

At 8 to 12 hours a week this is not a career move competing with 2070 Health. It is a
side engagement that runs alongside the search, and in that shape it does three things
nothing else on the board does.

**One — it builds the credential that has blocked seven lanes, and it does so in exactly
the form 2070 Health is asking for.**

Set the two postings side by side:

| Health Progress Hub | 2070 Health |
| --- | --- |
| *"Build or refine a weighted factor model for an organisation that wants to decide systematically where to expand"* | *"Evaluate portfolio attractiveness and strategic fit using defined business performance metrics"* |
| *"Carry out in-depth scoping of the strongest countries: disease burden, health system, regulation, partners, costs and operating conditions"* | *"Identify and size high-potential, underserved healthcare segments in India through rigorous primary and secondary research"* |
| *"Review the prioritization models and resources organisations use to score and compare countries, and the data behind them"* | *"Support prioritization of products and launches by providing fact-based insights and scenario analyses"* |

That is close to the same activity. Her geospatial heat map — every rupee across Indian
states, returns compared by geography and sub-theme — is already a weighted factor model
by another name. HPH would give her a **second, non-Tata reference** for that work, and a
current one.

**Being precise about what it does not fix.** A weighted factor model is not a DCF, a
comparables set, or a cap table. This would strengthen an answer to *"strong analytical
and quantitative modelling"*. It would **not** answer Artha's *"strong proof of financial
modeling"* on the buy-side, or 2070's *"proficiency in valuation"*. Those still need
something else. Overstating this would be the same mistake as calling the Tiff.in work a
food business.

**Two — speed, and a paid trial.** Ten days to the deadline, 48-hour review, offers inside
a week, and the test task is paid. That is the fastest process in the entire search by a
wide margin, and the only one where she gets paid to find out whether she wants it.

**Three — continuity.** Work that starts in weeks rather than months, at her own chosen
hours, without pausing anything else.

### The honest costs

- **Taken full-time, this is the trap.** Non-profit global health research, "Officer"
  title, reporting to a Director. That is a lateral into the sector she is leaving, and
  it would deepen the philanthropy framing rather than break it. **The part-time version
  is a tool; the full-time version is a step backwards.** This distinction is the whole
  recommendation.
- **On a CV, "Research & Scaling Officer" sitting under "Program Officer"** reads as more
  of the same to a commercial reader. Worth taking as a contract engagement, described as
  such, rather than as her new role.
- **The process costs about six hours** — form, 2.5-hour test task, two interviews, a live
  work test. For a part-time contract that is a real price, even with the test task paid.
- **Contractor terms.** Six months initially, no benefits, and the rate is published
  rather than negotiated from a band.
- **Pay.** $16–25/hour. At part-time hours this is supplementary, not replacement. Compare
  against her own numbers, which stay in `private/`.

### Recommendation

**Apply, before 15 October, and state 8 to 12 hours a week in the form.**

Not as a job, and not as a replacement for the commercial search. As the one thing on
this board that pays her to build the credential she keeps losing on, in the same ten days
that the 2070 Health conversation should be happening.

They ask applicants to say which areas interest them and to be honest where something
does not. Two specifics for her answer:

- **Prioritisation research is her lane** — say so plainly, and point at the geospatial
  work as the thing she has already done that most resembles it.
- **Research management is the honest stretch.** The posting asks for someone who can
  *"give written and verbal feedback to researchers and mentors who know their subject
  better than you do."* She does a version of this — she assessed founders and programme
  leaders on domain knowledge she did not hold — but she has not managed researchers.
  They explicitly invite that kind of honesty, and a candid answer will read better than
  a confident one.

**One thing to check before applying**, because it is the only gate still open: the
posting says *"remote, any time zone"*, which clears her constraint on its face. Confirm
in the first conversation that the **meeting** rhythm is also time-zone neutral. A
three-person team spread across continents usually means asynchronous work with a few
fixed calls, but the current Global Health Project Sprint runs 5 October to 15 November
and the mentors include senior officials across African governments and US-based charity
founders. Her timezone constraint has been absolute since day one, and "any time zone" in
a posting is a statement about where she sits, not about when the calls are.

## Pattern worth naming across all three

All three arrived together, and two of the three died or wobbled on something that has
nothing to do with whether she can do the work: **Denmark**, and **a sector label**.

The one that survived is the one that is **remote, part-time, written by a three-person
team describing a job rather than filtering a queue, and reviewed in 48 hours.**

That is now the fourth independent confirmation of the same finding: the roles she can
actually get are the ones where a human wrote the posting. It held for HospiPal, for the
ACT Fellowship, for Weekday, for 2070 Health, and it holds here.
