# The female-investors list — assessed individually

Evaluated 2026-10-08, from a LinkedIn post Nikita sent listing ten female investors.
She asked for an independent read on each and whether any are worth cold outreach for a
role.

**Verdict: none of the ten.** Not because they are not good investors, but because
**every single one of them is US, Canadian or Spanish, and none of them invest in India.**

That is a 10-for-10 failure on the two gates that have already closed her two
highest-scoring roles of the entire search: **Rock Health (FIT 4.6) and Paul Angell**, both
killed by **work authorisation**, and Rock Health also by the **timezone** constraint she
has held absolutely since day one.

### The pattern worth naming

Several of these funds have theses **tied to a specific country's underrepresented
founders** — women of colour in the US, the Latinx community, Canadian women's innovation.
A fund whose entire LP story is about backing a particular community *in a particular
country* hires inside that country and that community. That is not a criticism of the
funds; it is the reason this list cannot work for her, and it would not change however
good her note was.

### Anu Duggal is the one worth the extra paragraph, and it still fails

She has a genuine and interesting India history: she **opened India's first wine bar, The
Tasting Room, in 2005**, then co-founded **Exclusively.in**, a flash-sale site for Indian
artisans **backed by Accel and Tiger Global and acquired by Myntra in 2011**. That is a
real India operating record and a real connection point.

**But Female Founders Fund invests in US women-led technology companies.** Portfolio names
are Zola, Eloquii, Billie, Tala. **No Indian portfolio companies were found**, the fund is
New York based, and at roughly $100M AUM across its funds it is a small team.

So the honest position: she could write a warm, well-founded note to Anu Duggal and it
would be a reasonable note. **It would not produce a job**, because the fund has no India
presence, no India deals, and no reason to hire someone who needs a US visa.

**It is a LinkedIn engagement post.** It ends "Follow Keren Hod for more high-signal
insights" and "Which female investor should founders have on their radar?" It is written
for founders seeking capital, by someone growing a following. The investors are real; the
*list* was not assembled for anyone's job search, let alone one gated to India.

## And the reframe that matters more than any list

Her own search already established this, on 2026-09-07: **healthcare VC in India produced
zero postings across seven funds.** It is an outreach-only lane.

So cold-emailing an investor is not usually a route to a job. It is a route to a
conversation, an introduction, and market intelligence, which are worth having but are not
the same thing. **The exception is a fund with an actual staffing need**, and she has two
of those identified and unapproached:

- **2070 Health**, which has three business-side roles open at once
- **Farro Capital**, which is launching a $50M fund and asking for applications on a
  rolling basis

**Those are where the outreach hour goes.** Not to ten people in four countries she cannot
work in.

## What would be worth doing instead

If the intent behind the question is "are there female investors in my space I should know,"
that is a good question with a better answer than this list. **The useful search is
female-led or female-founded India healthcare and impact funds** — which is a real
category, is small enough to enumerate properly, and would clear every gate.

I have not researched it yet and will not guess at names. **Worth an hour if she wants it.**
Her existing brief on Kae Capital already shows the shape such research takes, and why it
works: it found that Sunitha Viswanathan had made the same crossing Nikita is making, from
capital deployed for impact to capital deployed for return. That is the kind of observation
that makes a cold note land, and it only comes from researching a short list properly
rather than reacting to a long one.
