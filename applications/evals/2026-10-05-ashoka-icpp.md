## The gate, and it is not a close call

**Sector gate: FAIL.** The healthtech/AI carve-out needs all three of its tests, and this
fails the first one outright: **healthcare is not the domain.** The programme is urban
development, municipal finance, property-tax data, government procurement and the
governance of algorithmic and digital systems. There is no health in it anywhere.

`RUBRIC.md` names this case explicitly in what stays out:

> General non-profit work, grant-making, foundation programme roles, and **policy or
> advocacy posts — including in healthcare.**

**The build-from-zero door also stays shut.** That exception needs the organisation or the
function not to exist yet. This programme already has a Senior Visiting Fellow, a faculty
co-lead, a research associate and consultants in place. She would be **running an existing
programme, not standing one up** — which is the case the rubric calls out: "Running a
portfolio inside a large foundation never qualifies, however senior the title."

### And then the plainest version

Strip the institution away and read the responsibilities: own a delivery calendar,
commission and publish policy briefs and working papers, run convenings, manage
government partnerships at the operational level, track budgets and deliverables against
plan, prepare periodic reporting, draft and edit outputs to a publishable standard.

**That is the job she is leaving.** It is programme delivery and policy writing inside a
mission-driven institution, reporting to a Director. The wrapper is a university instead
of a philanthropy, and the subject matter is cities instead of health. Nothing else about
it is different.

**Transition value scores 1.** Two years here and her CV reads "programme delivery at a
policy centre," which is further into the sector rather than out of it — and it is
*contractual*, so she would not even be buying security with the move.

## What the Ashoka advantage is actually for

This is the part worth taking away, because the connection is real and I don't want the
verdict to waste it.

**Ashoka is a referral asset for the commercial search, not a reason to go back to
Ashoka.** The Young India Fellowship is a recognised signal in Indian strategy,
consulting and investing hiring — it is already noted in the master resume as something to
keep visible. Its value is a warm introduction into IQVIA, Bridgespan, a fund, or a
founder's office. Spending it to return to the institution as a contractual programme
lead converts an asset into a lateral.

**The YIF network is the under-used thing on her board, and it is not under-used because
it's weak.** Eighteen months of her search has run through cold applications into
credential screens that killed nineteen of them. One warm introduction is worth more than
another application, and this is the network she has.
