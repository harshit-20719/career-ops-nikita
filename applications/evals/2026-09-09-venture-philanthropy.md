# Venture philanthropy — ACT and the rest of the lane

Researched 2026-09-09 at Nikita's request.

## The short version

**ACT is the most interesting organisation in this lane and the ACT Fellowship is the
wrong instrument for her.** Those are two separate findings and they point in different
directions.

---

## ACT

**ACT (ACT Capital Foundation for Social Impact)**, Bengaluru. Born out of the Indian
startup and venture ecosystem's Covid response, and now describes itself as having
**pioneered venture philanthropy in India**: deploying flexible, risk-tolerant capital to
innovation-first organisations, backing founders early. Their strapline is *"backing India's
next generation of impact unicorns."*

**Focus areas include health.** Registered as a Section 8 non-profit.

Why it matters for her specifically: ACT sits *inside* the VC ecosystem rather than
adjacent to it. It is staffed and funded by people from the startup and venture world. That
network is the asset, and it is a different network from the one four years at Tata Trusts
has given her.

### The ACT Fellowship 2026–27 — clears every gate, and is still the wrong move

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Format | **Nine-month, full-time programme** |
| Stipend | **₹90,000 per month** (~₹10.8L annualised) |
| Locations | Mumbai, Delhi **or** Bengaluru — she could stay in Mumbai |
| Eligibility | Resident Indian, **graduate of any discipline**, **at least 2 years** full-time work experience |
| Advantage | *"Prior experience as a founder or in strategy consulting, investment banking, impact investing, or **the social sector** will be a strong advantage"* |
| Process | Rolling, interviews with ACT's leadership team |
| Contact | `core-pm@actgrants.in` |

**Read the eligibility line twice.** "Graduate of any discipline" and "at least 2 years"
means this is the **first opportunity in this entire search with no credential wall** — no
MBA, no CA, no B.Pharm, no tier-1 institution. And her social-sector background is listed
as an *advantage* rather than something to explain away. On odds alone this is roughly
**4.0**, the best on the board.

**And I would still not take it.** Four reasons, in order of weight:

1. **The programme's purpose is the opposite of her direction.** ACT describes it as a
   launchpad *for professionals looking to pivot into the impact space*. Nikita is already
   in the impact space and trying to leave. She would be spending nine months and a salary
   cut to enter a room she is standing in.
2. **It is a nine-month fellowship, not a job.** She would leave a permanent role for a
   fixed-term programme with no guaranteed landing.
3. **₹90,000 a month is roughly a third below her current fixed pay.** She has no comp
   floor and money is not her axis, but this is a quantified cut for a temporary, junior
   position rather than a trade for scope.
4. **"Fellow" is a step down from Program Officer running a ₹450 crore portfolio**, and it
   is still philanthropy, so it does not solve the transition-value problem either.

**Estimated FIT ~2.5 / ODDS ~4.0.** The mirror image of her usual problem, and the fit side
is the one that matters here.

### What to do about ACT instead

**Go at their full-time roles, not the fellowship.** ACT has a careers page and open
positions; the listings sit behind a PyjamaHR portal that returns nothing to automated
reading, and ACT posts nothing to LinkedIn, so **she needs to open
`actgrants.in/careers-at-act/` herself.** That is a two-minute job for her and impossible
for me.

If nothing suitable is listed, `core-pm@actgrants.in` is a published address and ACT is
exactly the kind of organisation where a direct note works — small, ecosystem-embedded, and
used to people approaching them.

## The honest framing

Venture philanthropy is a genuinely distinct discipline — it applies investment method to
grant capital, and the good ones use instruments beyond grants. It is a **real bridge**, and
ACT is the best-positioned example in India because of whose ecosystem it sits in.

But it is still philanthropy. Taking a venture philanthropy role has the same
transition-value cost already recorded against the Bansal foundation and Bridgespan: it
does not credential her out of the sector she is leaving. It buys a network and a method,
not a change of label.

**Where that leaves it:** ACT is worth a look at their real roles and worth a direct email.
The fellowship is not worth nine months and a pay cut to re-enter a sector she is trying to
exit.
