# Where the search should point now

Written 2026-09-07, after Nikita asked whether to broaden beyond strategy roles into
early investment roles and social impact consulting.

Short answer: **yes, and in a specific direction.** Not all of the widening she proposed
survives contact with the requirements, and one part of it fails for exactly the reason
the last twelve roles failed.

---

## The distinction that reorganises everything

Nikita is leaving philanthropy because she wants the feedback loop that comes with
commercial outcomes. That is the real objective, and "for-profit" was only ever a proxy
for it.

The proxy has been wrong at least twice now. The sharper line is:

> **Grant-making deploys capital and measures impact. Investing deploys capital and gets
> money back — and finds out whether it was right.**

An impact fund or a development finance institution is on the *investing* side of that
line. The capital expects a financial return, the portfolio is marked, and being wrong
costs something. That is the loop she is looking for, and it is genuinely different from
the job she has, even when the mandate is social.

This is what the World Bank eval flagged on 13 August and nobody acted on: *"If the sector
gate was drawn around grant-making non-profits rather than every non-corporate employer,
development-finance institutions would be back in scope and would fit the stated goal
well."* That was right, and it should have redirected the search then.

## The three lanes, ranked

### Lane 1 — Impact investing and development finance · **strongest**

**Here her four years is the credential, not the thing she has to explain.**

Every mainstream VC role has rejected her on a version of "you have not closed deals."
Artha was explicit: *"must have closed at least 2 investments end-to-end on the buy-side"*
and *"candidates with only investment banking, transaction advisory, or consulting
backgrounds will not be considered."* That is not a gate framing can cross.

Impact funds and DFIs screen differently. They need people who can assess an organisation,
a management team and a theory of change, verify what is actually happening in the field,
and hold a portfolio to account after the cheque. That is a literal description of her job:
30–50 proposals a month against a fixed framework, founder and institutional-leader
assessment modelled on pre-investment diligence, cost-effectiveness analysis across
geographies, field visits to the remotest districts in India, and financial gatekeeping on
every partner budget in the theme.

**Live now:** Lightroad — *Analyst, Impact Finance* and *Manager, Strategic Partnerships*,
both New Delhi. ADB — *Investment Officer*, New Delhi (previously flagged and never scored).

**Target list, no posting required:** Aavishkaar Capital · Omnivore · Ankur Capital ·
Menterra · Social Alpha · Unitus Ventures · Caspian Debt · Northern Arc · Elevar Equity.
DFIs with India presence: British International Investment · FMO · Proparco · IFC · DFC ·
Norfund.

### Lane 2 — Healthcare VC · **best fit, worst access**

Domain depth plus diligence, which is the combination almost nobody else brings. The
problem is structural: these are small teams that rarely post.

**HealthQuad** is the obvious target — India's leading healthcare-focused VC, ~$280M AUM,
Delhi, 14 portfolio companies. Their posted investment-team role covers *"new deal
evaluation, portfolio monitoring and fund raise related initiatives"*, which is her work
almost word for word. **But it is framed as a pre-MBA role at 2–3 years**, which is below
her band.

That makes this a cold-outreach lane, not an application lane — the same play that
produced the Cent and Kae Capital briefs, and it works precisely because there is no
screen to fail.

**Targets:** HealthQuad · W Health Ventures · Somerset Indus Capital (Worli, Mumbai) ·
Quadria Capital · InvAscent / India Life Sciences Fund · Chiratae (health vertical).

### Lane 3 — Social impact consulting · **one yes, one no**

She named Dalberg and Bridgespan. They give opposite answers.

**Dalberg — closed, and for the same reason as the last twelve.**

- *Analyst* (Mumbai, New Delhi) is an entry-level role for recent graduates. The
  application asks for **SAT/ACT scores or GPA and a university transcript**. Applications
  closed **5 September 2026**. She is four years too senior for it.
- *Consultant* runs through the **Postgraduate Consultant Program** and requires a
  full-time MBA, MPA or MPP in progress.

Both doors are credential-gated. Dalberg is not the exception to the pattern — it is
another instance of it.

**Bridgespan — the best credential match found anywhere in this search.**

Their Consultant requirement, verbatim in substance:

> 2–6 years of professional experience, including at least two years in strategy
> consulting **or a reputed nonprofit or social sector impact organisation** or a strategy
> role with a conglomerate.

And the preferred education: *a postgraduate qualification (e.g. MBA, MPA, MPP, **PGD**)*.

Read that twice. **Her exact background is one of the three named qualifying paths**, and
**PGD is on the preferred list** — she holds a P.G.D. in Liberal Studies from Ashoka. She
is at 4y2m, inside the 2–6 band. After twelve rejections on credentials she does not have,
this is a posting whose requirements were effectively written around what she does have.

Mumbai and Delhi offices, three days a week in office, 50–55 hour weeks.

**The caveat:** reporting indicates the 2026 Associate Consultant and Consultant cycle is
complete. Whether a 2027 cycle is open has to be checked directly — that is the first
action, not a research project.

**Also live in this lane:** Sattva Consulting — *Senior Consultant, CSR Advisory*, Mumbai.
Others worth watching: FSG · Intellecap · Samagra · IPE Global · MicroSave.

## The honest tension, stated once

Lanes 1 and 3 keep her in the impact world. She has said twice she wants out of it, and
that objection deserves a straight answer rather than a quiet reframing.

- **Lane 1 answers it.** Deploying capital that expects a return is the feedback loop.
  A DFI or an impact fund is an investor. Her CV would read as a move to the buy side.
- **Lane 3 does not fully answer it.** Bridgespan is a non-profit advising non-profits and
  foundations. What it buys is a *consulting* credential and an alumni network that moves
  into impact investing, foundations and corporate strategy — a genuine launchpad rather
  than a lateral. That is the same argument used for the Bansal foundation role, and it
  carries the same cost: transition value scores low, and it should be stated in the score
  rather than argued away.

If she wants one lane, it is Lane 1. If she wants the fastest credible offer, Lane 3.

## What to stop doing

**Large pharma, medtech and life-sciences consulting strategy roles.** 0-for-8, all on a
degree line, documented in `2026-09-07-degree-gates.md`. Each costs an hour and returns a
filter.

**Mainstream generalist VC and PE associate roles.** Artha, Navi and Peak XV all rejected
the profile on stated requirements — closed deals, term-sheet negotiation, or sector
domain. Impact and healthcare funds are where the same ambition is reachable.
