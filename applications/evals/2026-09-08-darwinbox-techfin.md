## The strongest case for applying, which the first pass dismissed too fast

This deserves airing properly, because it is a better argument than I gave it credit for.

**Financial modelling is the single recurring reason her investment applications die.**

- **ADB, Investment Officer** — closed on "independently prepare financial models,"
  sensitivity analysis and stress testing
- **Artha Group, Associate** — "MBA or CA required **OR strong proof of financial
  modeling**, AOP structuring, and decision-prep ownership"
- **Navi, Head of Corporate Development** — DCF and trading/transaction multiples
- **Millennium, Research Analyst** — financial modelling in Bloomberg, Visible Alpha,
  Canalyst

Four rejections, one gap. And her own master resume has flagged it as unresolved since
11 August.

**A technology finance seat would fix that gap directly**, at a unicorn, in a P0 city, in a
band that matches her years. Owning cloud unit economics and driver-based forecasting is
not a detour from commercial judgement, it is the commercial-judgement muscle she has never
been made to build. Two years of it and the Artha-shaped rejection stops happening.

That is a real argument and it is why transition value could defensibly score 4 rather
than 3, which would put FIT at 3.3.

## Why it still does not work

**She cannot get this job.** The steelman is about whether the role is *good for her*.
ODDS is about whether she can *have* it, and 2.0 is the honest number.

"Education: CA / MBA Finance" appears under **Required** Skills & Experience, not under
Preferred. In Indian finance hiring that is a screening filter, applied before anyone reads
the experience section. It is a different thing from Bellatrix's "6–10 years" or IQVIA's
"tier-1 preferred" — those are bars to argue against. This is a qualification checkbox.

And unlike the healthcare roles where she clears the substance and loses on paperwork, here
she also fails the substance: no FP&A background, no modelling, no ERP, no SaaS domain, no
cloud or LLM cost exposure. There is no framing that closes four gaps at once.

**The build-from-zero exception does not rescue it either.** "Build a best-in-class
Technology Finance function" is real, but that exception was written to override a *sector*
gate, not to override a credential and skills mismatch. Stretching it here would be
bending the rubric to reach a conclusion.

---

## The useful conclusion

**The insight is right and the vehicle is wrong.**

If financial modelling is the thing blocking her investment lane — and the evidence says it
is, four times over — the answer is not a finance job she cannot get. It is to close the
gap directly and cheaply:

1. **Learn it deliberately.** A structured financial modelling course, then build two real
   models: a three-statement model, and a DCF on a healthcare company she can actually talk
   about. That is weeks, not years, and it costs nothing but evenings.
2. **Then re-approach the roles that rejected her on it.** Artha's line was "MBA or CA
   required **OR strong proof of financial modelling**." That "or" is a door, and it is the
   only one of those four rejections with an explicit alternative route written into it.
3. **Update the master resume's "To confirm" block** once it is real. It has been flagged
   as unresolved for four weeks and it is now demonstrably the most expensive gap in the
   search.

That gets her the same capability, keeps her pointed at strategy and investing rather than
FP&A, and does not require winning a job whose first filter she fails.

**Recommendation: do not apply. Do fix the gap.**
