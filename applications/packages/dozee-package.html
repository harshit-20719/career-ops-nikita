04

They will ask

“Tell us about your exposure to AI products and AI-led workflow automation.”

This is the one real gap and it is explicitly in the requirements, so it will be asked. Do not overclaim. The honest and still-strong answer: you have spent four years as the person deciding which AI health technologies deserved funding, including AI diagnostics as an owned sub-theme, and you structured their pilots under a deliberately heavier evidence bar. Then be concrete about your own tooling — what you actually use AI for in analysis, reporting and tracking. Have one real example. A vague answer here is the likeliest way to lose this.

“What was the 60% improvement, and how was it measured?”

They asked for ‘demonstrated ability to deliver measurable improvements’, so this number is why your CV gets read — and it is the one figure on it you cannot currently explain precisely. It is flagged in your own master resume as unverified. Work out before the interview what the baseline was and what was measured, or stop using the number. An unexplainable metric in a role about executive reporting is worse than no metric.

"Describe running three or more cross-functional programmes at once."

Their requirement almost verbatim. Your answer is the dual mandate: 40+ concurrent programmes, two roles, screening running alongside design cycles. Name the tracking mechanism, not just the volume — what the dashboard contained, how often it refreshed, and one case where it surfaced a problem before the partner reported it.

“How do you handle a CxO priority that conflicts with another function’s plan?”

The real test of a founder’s office seat, and they named ‘resolve dependencies, surface risks’ in the JD. Answer with a mechanism rather than diplomacy: how you would surface the conflict with a written option set and a recommendation, rather than brokering it quietly. Discretion is also a stated requirement, so show you know the difference between confidential and invisible.

"Why move from a philanthropic institution to a startup?"

Expect scepticism about pace. Pre-empt it: name that you are coming from an institution rather than a startup, that you would be adjusting to their velocity rather than arriving used to it, and then point at what transfers — the habit of owning the cadence rather than waiting to be asked.

05

Ask them

  1. Whose office is this exactly — the CEO’s, or the CxO group collectively? Who sets my priorities when they conflict?
  2. What are the two or three programmes this role would own in the first quarter?
  3. You are in 300+ hospitals across four countries. Which of those markets is the growth priority, and what is the constraint?
  4. What does “AI and product fluency” look like in practice day to day — is it using the tools, or engaging with the roadmap?
  5. What happened to the person who did this before, or is it a new seat?
  6. Six months in, what would have to be true for this to have worked?

Hold two back for a later round — they will ask again.
