04

They will ask

“What do you know about GLP-1s and the obesity market in India?”

Prepare this properly or do not apply. It is the one question you cannot improvise, and your portfolio did not include diabetes, obesity or metabolic health — do not imply otherwise. Learn the actual shape: who the Indian players are, what semaglutide going off-patent in India means for volume, why adherence collapses after month three, and what a pharma company is actually buying when it funds a support programme. Then connect it to what you do know: you have spent four years on why proven interventions fail to reach people.

"Have you negotiated a commercial contract?"

No, and say so in one clause rather than hedging. You have structured fifteen-plus partnership agreements including co-funding terms, and you were the financial gatekeeper who evaluated every partner budget in the health theme before it reached CEO and Board approval. That is commercial judgement applied from the other side of the table. Then say what you would want in your first month — sight of two existing pharma contracts and a conversation with whoever has run one.

“Why are you leaving a ₹450 crore portfolio for this?”

The honest answer is the strong one: the portfolio is scale without a feedback loop. You recommend, capital goes out, and what comes back is a report rather than a result. A partnership with a pharma company has a contract, a KPI and a renewal decision. Do not dress this up as passion for obesity care — they will see through it, and the structural answer is better.

"Walk us through a partnership you built from nothing."

Near-certain, and you have fifteen to choose from. Pick one where you found the counterparty yourself, name what they wanted that you had to construct, what nearly killed it, and what the governance looked like after signature. The post-signature half is where most candidates stop talking and where this role actually lives.

"How would you design a patient support programme for a new GLP-1 launch?"

Scenario question, and your programme-design record answers it. Two-to-three month design cycles built from the ground up with the delivery partner, multi-stakeholder dependency mapping, and designing for continuity beyond the funding period. Say what you would measure in week one and what evidence would make you stop.

Ask them

  1. How many pharma partnerships are live today, and what does the pipeline look like for the next year?
  2. Who owns the commercial relationship today, and what changes when this role exists?
  3. On a patient support programme, what does the pharma partner actually hold you accountable for — enrolment, adherence, or outcomes?
  4. How do Product and Clinical get involved, and where does that handoff usually break?
  5. The title says founder’s office but the job is partnerships. Which is it in practice, and who do I report to?
  6. What has Healthify tried in pharma partnerships that did not work?

Hold two back for a later round — they will ask again.
