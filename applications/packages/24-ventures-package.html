04

They will ask

“Walk me through your valuation experience.”

The question that decides this application, and the answer must not bluff. Say plainly that you have not built a DCF or a comparables set, then say what you have built: driver-based budgets, unit cost against plan, variance analysis, and the Tiff.in projections. Then make the real argument: origination and appraisal are about whether a business is worth looking at, and valuation is a later question in the process you are being hired for the front of. The posting itself scopes you as supporting the investment team through the early stages of the deal lifecycle.

“How would you source for a sector-agnostic platform?”

They are sector agnostic and you are a healthcare specialist, so this will be probed. Answer with method rather than domain: the 20 to 30 parameter screening framework, how you decided what to look at when 30 to 50 arrived a month, and the geospatial work that showed the same model producing different returns by geography. Then be straight that healthcare is where your network is and that it is a starting point rather than the whole map.

"You have never worked at a fund. Why should we take you over someone from PE or banking?"

Do not fight it on their ground. The honest differentiator: you have sat on the committee side of the table, not the deck-preparation side. Four years of writing the recommendation that a board either approved or did not, and of defending it. Most junior finance candidates have built models for someone else to defend.

"What have you turned down, and why?"

A sourcing role is a declining role, and this is the question where your record is unusually strong. You screened well over a thousand proposals and funded a fraction. Have two specific examples ready: one you declined that looked good on paper, and one where you set a heavier evidence bar and the pilot answered the question.

"Why leave philanthropy for investing?"

Keep it factual and short, the way the Beyond letter does. You want to do the same judgement where the capital expects a return. Do not disparage the Trusts work — the four years are the entire reason you are a credible candidate.

05

Ask them

  1. What does the pipeline look like today, and where does most of it come from — inbound, bankers or your own network?
  2. Who writes the screening note and who takes it to the committee? I want to know whether this seat has a voice in the decision or only in the pipeline.
  3. How sector-agnostic is it in practice? What have you actually invested in over the last eighteen months?
  4. Cheque size, stage and typical hold period?
  5. Is the fund SEBI-registered as an AIF, and which fund would I be sourcing for?
  6. Who would I report to, and how many people are on the investment team?
  7. What would make the first six months a success in your view?

Hold two back for a later round — they will ask again.

06

Know before the interview

  • Valuation is asked for explicitly — “preliminary financial analysis and valuation assessment” and “understanding of valuation methodologies”. This is the eleventh lane where financial modelling appears, and unlike Makers Hive this one wants the valuation half specifically, which is the half you have never touched.
  • You have none of the six preferred backgrounds. AIF, VC, PE, investment banking, corporate VC, investment or transaction advisory. It is a soft screen rather than a gate, but with 200 applicants in a day it will get used.
  • Tagged “entry level” against a 3–10 year band, which is a mismatch inside their own posting. It may mean the seat is junior, or that whoever posted it was careless. Worth asking what the title actually is before accepting anything.
  • Sector-agnostic cuts against you. Your depth is healthcare and it stops being a differentiator here; the heat map, the screening framework and the diligence discipline are what transfer.
  • No named contact and no careers page, so there is no referral path to build and nothing to verify the firm against beyond the posting. Check their registration and portfolio independently before a second conversation.
  • The upside is why it is worth an hour anyway: a posted origination seat in Bengaluru whose qualification line says “preferred” rather than “required” is exactly the door this search has spent two months failing to find in venture.
