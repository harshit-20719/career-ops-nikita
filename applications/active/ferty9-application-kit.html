The 300-word write-up

Their required question —

When Ferty9 decides where its next centre goes, that is a geography-level capital allocation question — and it is the question I spent two years answering at Tata Trusts.

I built a geospatial investment heat map covering every rupee the Trusts deployed across India, measuring how identical interventions produced materially different returns depending on geography and terrain. It shaped portfolio allocation from 2022 to 2024. Applied to a clinic network, the discipline is the same: which markets justify a centre, what returns to expect there, and where capital is better held back.

The rest maps closely too. I manage a ₹450 crore ($51M) healthcare portfolio across 40+ programmes, screening 30–50 proposals a month and taking recommendations directly to the CEO and Board. I assess founders and institutional leaders on whether they can genuinely execute and deploy capital — the same judgement a network applies to the doctors and partners it bets on. And I built the Trusts' climate-health vertical from nothing: the thesis, the first programmes, and the funding to stand it up. I know what it takes to start a line of business inside an organisation that did not previously have one.

What I'd bring that is less common: I've evaluated healthcare delivery in the field, including in districts that barely appear on the map — not from a deck. I've assessed a district hospital and an AI diagnostics founder in the same month. That range means I can move between a clinical conversation and a growth model without a translator.

I am deliberately moving from philanthropic capital into commercial healthcare, because I want the feedback loop that comes with it. Ferty9 — investor-backed, expanding, and a month into a new CEO — is where that decision compounds fastest.

Every claim here traces to something you told me. If you'd rather not name the ₹450 crore figure externally, swap it for "a $51M healthcare portfolio" and the paragraph still works.

If they call — prep

Company brief

  • What they are: South India fertility-care network — 11 centres across Andhra Pradesh and Telangana. Named Best IVF Chain in South India, 2024.
  • Backing: Verlinvest, a growth-stage consumer investor. Implies an expansion mandate and eventual exit pressure.
  • CEO: Sukesh Chandra Gain, since July 2026. Previously Chief Business Officer at Birla Fertility & IVF; before that ran revenue and operations for 50+ IVF clinics across 35 cities. He thinks in unit economics and network expansion — pitch to that.
  • Read before the call: the Indian IVF market's growth drivers, what drives a fertility centre's economics (cycles per centre, doctor productivity, patient acquisition cost), and how Birla, Indira IVF and Nova compete.

Questions you'll get

"Why leave Tata Trusts?"

Your prepared answer, rehearsed. Forward-looking, no criticism, 30 seconds. Full script in private/exit-narrative.md.

"You've worked in philanthropy. Why should we believe you'll adapt to a commercial P&L?"

The honest answer is strongest: you already allocate capital against returns, you just measured return in impact rather than revenue. The heat map is ROI analysis. Add the Tiff.in unit-economics work as evidence you've modelled a commercial business.

"How would you decide where we open our next centre?"

Almost certain to be asked, and it's your question. Structure it: demand signals and demographics, competitive density, doctor availability, referral catchment, payer mix, cannibalisation of existing centres, payback period. Then say what you'd need to see in the data before committing — that restraint is what separates a strategist from an analyst.

"What's your current and expected compensation?"

The JD says compensation isn't a constraint for the right candidate — so don't anchor yourself first. "My current fixed is ₹16 lakh, which is a philanthropic-sector band rather than a market one. What range have you budgeted?"

Ask them

  1. What does the CEO want to be true in 12 months that isn't true today?
  2. Is the growth thesis primarily new centres, or more throughput from the existing eleven?
  3. How does Verlinvest's involvement shape the decision-making cadence?
  4. What's the hardest strategic call the leadership team is sitting on right now?

Hold two back for a later round — they will ask again.
