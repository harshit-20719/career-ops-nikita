# Where to find roles — India

Ordered by how well they convert, not by how many listings they carry. Under a short
timeline, effort spent at the top of this list is worth several times the same effort at
the bottom.

## Tier 1 — referrals and direct

Nothing else comes close. A referred application gets read by a human; a portal
application competes with several hundred others for the same glance.

- **People you already know.** Start with `contacts/contacts.csv`. Before applying
  anywhere, grep it for the company.
- **Second-degree via LinkedIn.** Filter your connections by company. A former colleague
  of a former colleague is a warmer path than any job board.
- **Company careers pages directly.** For tier A targets, apply on their site rather
  than through an aggregator — fewer applicants and the ATS record is cleaner.
- **Alumni networks.** College and past-employer groups. Underused and unusually
  responsive.

## Tier 2 — high-signal platforms

- **LinkedIn Jobs** — the primary board for mid-to-senior roles in India. Turn on
  "Open to work" (recruiters-only visibility if the search is confidential). Sort by
  most recent and apply within 48 hours of posting; the first tranche of applicants gets
  materially more attention.
- **Instahyre** — curated, strong for product and engineering roles at funded startups.
  Recruiters approach you.
- **Cutshort** — startup-weighted, decent matching, low noise.
- **Wellfound (AngelList Talent)** — startups, often with comp ranges published up front.
- **Hirist** — tech-focused, good Bangalore and Hyderabad coverage.

## Tier 3 — volume boards

Useful for discovering that a company is hiring at all; apply through their own site
once you've found the role.

- **Naukri** — the widest coverage in India by a distance. Keep the profile updated;
  recruiters search it constantly. Expect a high volume of poorly-matched calls.
- **Indeed India**
- **Foundit** (formerly Monster India)

## Tier 4 — worth a standing check

- **YC company job boards** — `workatastartup.com`, many with India-based teams
- **Company Slack and Discord communities** in your domain
- **Twitter/X hiring threads** — noisy, but fast, and often pre-posting
- **Recruiters who specialize in your function** — one good one is worth a board

## Working the boards under time pressure

- **Set up alerts** on LinkedIn, Instahyre, and Naukri for each target title, delivered
  daily. Reacting to a two-week-old posting is close to pointless.
- **Apply within 48 hours** of a posting going up.
- **Never apply cold when a referral path exists.** Spend the extra day finding the
  person — the conversion difference is not close.
- **Log everything** in `applications/applications.csv` the same day. Under volume, an
  untracked application is one you'll either forget or duplicate.

## A note on recruiter calls

Indian recruiters typically open with current CTC, expected CTC, and notice period,
sometimes within the first two minutes. Have all three answers ready and rehearsed —
being caught unprepared on the comp question early is the most common way candidates
anchor themselves low. `outreach/README.md` and the comp section of your intake answers
are where those numbers live.
