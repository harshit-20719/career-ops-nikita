# Wording reference — the investment register, and honest alternatives

Written 2026-10-06, after Nikita flagged that describing a philanthropy in
capital-deployment language is misleading.

**She is right, and the reason matters more than the wording.** From `CLAUDE.md`: an
overstatement "survives until an interviewer asks a follow-up question." The specific
follow-up this invites is *"what returns did you generate?"* — and on a grant portfolio
there is no financial answer. Losing a Healthify or Dozee interview to that question would
be entirely self-inflicted.

But the fix is narrower than it first looks. **Most of the CV is already accurate.** Four
or five phrases are doing the damage.

---

## Where it actually appears

Across 23 resume versions: `capital deployed` in 21, `returns` in 23, `deploy capital` in
18, `investment heat map` in 18, `pre-investment diligence` in 18, `ROI` in 16,
`investment recommendations` in 13, `investment briefs` in 13, `deal lifecycle` in 9.

## What is already honest — do not change these

| Phrase | Why it is fine |
| --- | --- |
| **"portfolio"** | Standard philanthropic usage. Foundations run portfolios |
| **"$51M (₹450 crore) healthcare portfolio"** | True |
| **"Screened 30–50 proposals per month against a 20–30 parameter framework"** | True and register-neutral |
| **"budget allocation, utilization tracking, variance analysis"** | True and neutral |
| **"cost-per-beneficiary efficiency"** | True, and honestly philanthropic |
| **"Originated and closed 15+ institutional partnerships… structuring terms"** | True |
| **"Board of Trustees"** | True, and signals philanthropy honestly rather than hiding it |

## The two that are corrections, not choices

### 1. The word "grant" was dropped, and it should go back

Her **confirmed** master wording, marked "use this figure, in this wording, on every
version":

> Managed a **₹450 crore ($51M) healthcare grant portfolio** across 40+ programmes

`base-generalist.html` renders it as "healthcare portfolio" — **the word "grant" was lost
somewhere in the build, so the CV is less accurate than her own master.**

**This is the single highest-leverage fix in this document.** One word, placed early,
sets the register for everything downstream. Once a reader knows it is a grant portfolio,
"capital deployed" and "portfolio allocation" are plainly honest rather than borrowed.

### 2. "ROI" is factually wrong on a grant portfolio

The heat-map bullet currently reads "analyzing how identical program interventions
produced different **ROI** across geographies… and what **returns** to expect by region."

ROI came from Nikita's own intake, so it is not an invention — but a return on investment
is a financial ratio, and there was no financial return. **The real term of art for what
she built is cost-effectiveness analysis**, which is a recognised discipline in health
economics and is *stronger* with a healthcare audience than ROI, not weaker. Healthify,
Dozee and 2070 Health would all read "cost-effectiveness by geography" as a real skill.

| Was | Becomes |
| --- | --- |
| "produced different ROI across geographies and terrains" | "produced materially different **cost-effectiveness** across regions and terrains" |
| "what returns to expect by region" | "what **results** to expect by region" |
| "scoring and comparing **returns** by geography and sub-theme" | "scoring and comparing **outcomes per rupee** by geography and sub-theme" |

## The three that are her choice — three registers

For each, A is the plainest, B is neutral-but-commercial, C is the current wording.

### "translating directly to programs initiated and capital deployed"

- **A.** "translating directly to programmes funded and grants disbursed"
- **B.** "translating directly to programmes initiated and **funding committed**"
- **C.** keep "capital deployed"

**Recommended: B.** "Funding committed" is exact, carries no return implication, and still
reads commercially. **C also becomes defensible once "grant portfolio" is restored** — she
did deploy ₹450 crore; the only ambiguity was what kind of capital, and the first bullet
now answers that.

### "delivered investment recommendations to the CEO and Board of Trustees"

- **A.** "delivered **funding recommendations** to the CEO and Board of Trustees"
- **B.** "delivered **funding recommendations** to the CEO and Board of Trustees, **the body
  approving every commitment**"
- **C.** keep "investment recommendations"

**Recommended: B.** It is more accurate *and* stronger — "the body approving every
commitment" tells a reader the recommendations carried real weight, which "investment"
only implies.

### "Developed investment briefs and portfolio dashboards presented directly to the CEO"

- **A.** "Developed **funding briefs** and performance dashboards"
- **B.** "Developed **decision briefs** and performance dashboards"
- **C.** keep "investment briefs"

**Recommended: B.** "Decision briefs" is accurate, register-neutral, and describes the
function rather than borrowing a label.

## One more worth changing on accuracy grounds

> "applying **pre-investment diligence standards** to every proposal advanced"

The master says this work was *"explicitly modelled on pre-investment diligence"* — which
is a **comparison Nikita drew**. On the CV it reads as an assertion that she applied
investment-grade due diligence, which is a different claim.

**Better, and more concrete:**

> "assessing capability, domain expertise, and **capacity to manage funds at a scale they
> had not handled before**"

That loses the borrowed credibility and gains a specific, checkable observation. It is
also the more interesting sentence.

---

## What stays true after all of this

Worth stating, because the point is not to shrink the CV:

**Nothing about the substance changes.** She screened 30–50 proposals a month against a
fixed framework, ran diligence on the survivors, assessed the people behind them, took
recommendations to a CEO and a Board that approved every commitment, monitored 40+
programmes against plan, and built the model that redirected allocation for two years.
**That is the same work whether the capital was grant or equity** — and the master
resume's own positioning note says exactly that: "this is investment work in a
philanthropic wrapper."

The difference is that the CV should let a reader draw that conclusion rather than assert
it for them. **An interviewer who works it out themselves is convinced. One who is told
and then finds a gap is not.**

## For the record

Keep the positioning framing for *conversations and cover notes*, where she can give
context and answer follow-ups. Keep it off the CV, which is read without her in the room.
