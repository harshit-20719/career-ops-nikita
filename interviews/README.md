# Interviews

Three things live here:

| Path | When you touch it |
| --- | --- |
| `prep/behavioral-stories.md` | Once, then refine forever — the STAR bank |
| `prep/company-research-TEMPLATE.md` | Copy per company, before the first call |
| `debriefs/TEMPLATE.md` | Copy per round, within an hour of finishing |

## The rhythm

**Before:** company research from the template, re-read the job description in
`applications/active/<id>.md`, pick the three or four stories from the bank most likely
to fit, and write your questions for them.

**During:** take rough notes. Names and titles of everyone you meet, and anything they
say about timeline, team structure, or what the role is really about.

**After — the same day:** fill in a debrief. Memory for this decays within hours and it
is the single highest-value habit in the whole process. Every question they asked, how
you answered, what you'd change.

Then update the CSV row: `status`, `next_action`, `next_action_date`.

## Why debriefs matter

Companies reuse questions, and so does the industry. By your fifth loop you'll have seen
most of what's coming, but only if you wrote it down. The debriefs also make the thank-you
note specific, which is the only thing that makes it worth sending.

## Standing prep

- **The story bank** in `prep/behavioral-stories.md` — 8–12 stories covering the common
  themes, rehearsed enough to be fluent, not memorized.
- **"Tell me about yourself"** — 90 seconds, rewritten per role. Where you've been, what
  you do well, why this role. Practice it out loud. It opens nearly every interview and
  it sets the frame for everything after.
- **"Why are you leaving?"** — a version that's honest and doesn't disparage anyone.
  Get this one right before you need it.
- **Salary** — know your number and your walk-away before anyone asks. Deflect early,
  and let them name a range first if you can.
