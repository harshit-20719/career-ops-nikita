# Story bank

8–12 stories, each in STAR form, covering the themes below. Most behavioral questions
are one of these wearing a different hat, so a good story gets reused across themes —
the framing changes, the facts don't.

Rehearse until you can tell each one in about two minutes without notes. Fluent, not
memorized: a recited answer is obvious and it kills the conversation.

## Themes to cover

- [ ] Something you shipped that mattered
- [ ] A conflict with a colleague
- [ ] A failure, and what changed afterward
- [ ] Leading without formal authority
- [ ] A hard technical or strategic decision under uncertainty
- [ ] Handling ambiguity — no spec, no owner, unclear goal
- [ ] Disagreeing with a manager or a senior decision
- [ ] Mentoring or unblocking someone else
- [ ] A deadline you missed, or nearly did
- [ ] Feedback that was hard to hear

## Story: <short name>

**Themes it covers:** shipped something · leadership

- **Situation** — the context, in two sentences. What was at stake and why it was hard.
- **Task** — what specifically was yours to do. Be precise about the boundary between
  your work and the team's; interviewers probe this and vagueness reads as inflation.
- **Action** — what *you* did. This is the bulk of it. "We" is the word that sinks
  behavioral answers — say "I".
- **Result** — what changed. Numbers if you have them, and what you learned if the
  result was mixed.

**Follow-ups they'll ask:**
- What would you do differently?
- What did the rest of the team think?
- How did you know it worked?

## Notes on delivery

- Two minutes. Longer and you lose them; shorter usually means you skipped the Action.
- Name the outcome even when it's bad. A failure story with no real failure in it is
  worse than not having one.
- Have one number per story if the work allowed for one.
- If you don't have a story for a question, say so and offer the nearest real thing.
  Inventing one falls apart under two follow-ups.
